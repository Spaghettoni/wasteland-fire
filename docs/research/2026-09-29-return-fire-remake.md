# Building a Return Fire remake for home split-screen play: research note

> Later note (2026-09-29): the game's rules were replaced the same day by the Wasteland Fire rules in `design/rules.md` (three rock-paper-scissors units, a Water Canister per Base, Fuel Cans, unlimited respawn, no towers or turrets). Part 1 below documents the original Return Fire and is historical reference only; Part 2 on Godot building blocks still applies.

Date: 2026-09-29.

Scope: a private, non-distributed remake to play with friends at home. Trademark and licensing questions do not apply to that scope. The legal research from the first pass is kept in an appendix at the end in case plans ever change; nothing else in this note depends on it.

Question researched: "I'd like to create a game, a refreshed remake of Return Fire (capture the flag, split screen, Mad Max post-apocalyptic world). I'm not sure how to approach game development."

Method: two research passes against primary sources. Part 1 used the original game's manuals, help file and disc credits plus the designer's interviews. Part 2 used official engine documentation and release pages and the authors' own texts on game loops, patterns, prototyping, scope and playtesting. Every claim links to the source that owns it. Secondary sources are labelled "(secondary source)". Anything that could not be confirmed is marked **Unverified:**. Versions were checked on 2026-09-29 and will drift.

Location: the project folder had no notes convention (it was empty), so research notes live in `docs/research/`, one dated file per topic.

## Summary: how to approach it

### 1. Engine: Godot 4.7 with GDScript. Fallback: Unity 6.3 LTS

- Godot has every building block this game needs as a built-in node with an official demo: SubViewport and SubViewportContainer for split screen, InputEvent.device and the Input singleton for two or more gamepads, CharacterBody3D for arcade vehicles, GridMap for grid-based maps painted in the editor or filled from a JSON file, NavigationAgent3D for AI units, Area3D for flags, depots and hit zones. Its beginner path ends in a 3D game built on CharacterBody3D. The editor is one universal macOS binary needing 4 GB RAM, and scene files are plain text, so Git works normally. Details: Part 2, sections A and B.
- Unity 6.3 LTS is the fallback if you already know C# well or want PlayerInputManager's automatic join-and-split-screen. It has no built-in 3D grid map, so maps become hand-rolled prefab grids.
- Unreal 5.8 (16 GB RAM minimum on macOS, Xcode 26, binary assets, C++ and Blueprints) and Bevy (Rust, no editor, breaking releases roughly every three months) are poor fits for a solo newcomer on this game.

### 2. Movement: arcade kinematic bodies, not physics vehicles

- Godot's own VehicleBody3D reference says the class "has known issues", and every engine's physics-vehicle path is a suspension-and-tyre simulation an overhead arcade game does not need. Use CharacterBody3D with move_and_slide() for the jeep, tank and ASV; MOTION_MODE_FLOATING for the helicopter with altitude as a separate value; RigidBody3D only for debris and knocked-over props.
- Simulation (movement, firing, fuel, ammo, AI) runs in _physics_process at a fixed step; camera smoothing and HUD run in _process; enable physics interpolation early. Details: Part 2, sections A.3 and C.1.

### 3. Process: learn on the official tutorials, then build in milestones

1. Godot's Getting Started pages (Introduction, Step by step), then "Your first 2D game", then "Your first 3D game". The 3D tutorial itself recommends doing 2D first. Part 2, section D.1 lists every page in order.
2. Milestone 0: one vehicle on a flat plane with one gamepad. Stop when it is fun to drive for five minutes ("build the toy first").
3. Milestone 1: two players, split screen, two gamepads.
4. Milestone 2: flags, bases, capture and scoring with grey boxes. This is the vertical slice: one complete round of the real game. Put a friend on the sofa and watch what they do rather than what they say (Valve's playtesting talk).
5. Milestones 3 to 6: turrets and AI units; all four vehicles with fuel and ammo as data Resources; a grid map format and a minimal editor; destructibles, audio, menus. Milestone 7 shrinks to "export a build for the living-room machine"; skip the Steam part. Part 2, section E ties each milestone to its doc pages.
6. Guard rails: Git plus Git LFS from the first commit, using Godot's generated .gitignore and .gitattributes. Get a two-player, one-map, two-vehicle version playable before adding the rest (Derek Yu's "death loops" of restarting and polishing).

### 4. What Return Fire actually was (your design spec)

Two players on a split screen, each with an underground bunker holding up to three each of four vehicles: the helicopter (fast, two weapon systems, the only thing that clears mines, must return to the bunker to refuel), the tank (rotating turret, 19 turret hits to die), the ASV (heaviest, the only mine layer, 26 hits) and the jeep (fastest, dies to one hit, amphibious, the only vehicle that can carry a flag). The enemy flag is hidden in one of several towers; you destroy towers until you find it, drive the jeep to it and bring it home while the opponent does the same, and you can retrieve and re-hide your own flag. Depots refuel and rearm land vehicles; the bunker repairs everything. Turrets, homing drones, mines and an indestructible boundary submarine are the hazards. There is no AI commander, because the designer wanted "the computer as the board" for two humans; single player is training against fortifications. Everything is destructible, including bridges and trees. The camera is a fixed-heading 2.5D overhead view with zoom, chosen to keep split-screen above 12 fps on the 3DO. Full detail with manual citations: Part 1, sections B and G.

### 5. The music, as the original used it

Verified from the disc's own credits (Part 1, section C): Holst's "Mars" for the tank, Grieg's "In the Hall of the Mountain King" for the ASV, Wagner's "Ride of the Valkyries" for the helicopter, Rimsky-Korsakov's "Flight of the Bumblebee" for the jeep, Handel's "Hallelujah Chorus" when the flag is found, the finale of Rossini's "William Tell Overture" on pickup, and Verdi's "Dies Irae" on the title. The cue data shows one theme per vehicle with alternative start points, death stingers, a win cue and a bunker loop. For a home build, use whichever recordings you like; what to copy is that structure. The designer chose the loudest, most "rock 'n' roll" performances he could find.

### 6. Assets

Blender exports glTF Binary (.glb) that Godot imports directly. Kenney, Quaternius and Poly Haven publish free low-poly vehicles, city and industrial kits, textures and HDRIs that fit a wasteland look; a flat-shaded palette-texture style is cheap to make and cheap to render in split screen. Details: Part 2, section F.

### 7. Options worth deciding early

- Return Fire is two-player. If you want three or four friends on one screen, the SubViewport layout extends to four quadrants, but the rules need a team or free-for-all variant and the frame budget doubles. Decide before milestone 2.
- Godot exports Windows, macOS and Linux builds from your Mac. The macOS export page notes that unsigned builds are blocked by Gatekeeper when downloaded from unknown sources, so for a home build either run from the editor or keep the export local.

## Things to check by hand

- Whether Godot's Input Map UI can pin an action to a specific joypad device index. Otherwise filter on event.device in code, or define one action set per player.

## Suggested first week

1. Install Godot 4.7.2 (universal macOS build) and work through the two official tutorials listed in Part 2, section D.1.
2. Create the project, generate the version-control metadata, then `git init`, `git lfs install`, first commit.
3. Milestone 0: a grey box that drives well with one gamepad.
4. Plug in a second gamepad and start milestone 1.

Primary sources are the game's own printed/in-game materials, the developer's own words, publisher/platform-holder releases, USPTO TSDR records, US Copyright Office records, statute text, court opinions and the licence pages themselves. ## B. Core mechanics as a design reference

### Objective and win condition
- "In Return Fire, your main objective is to break down and destroy your enemy's defenses, capture the enemy's flag and bring it back safely to your bunker before your opponent captures your flag and brings it back to his bunker." ([Saturn manual, "Your Mission"](https://archive.org/download/Return_Fire_1997_Silent_Software_US/Return_Fire_1997_Silent_Software_US_djvu.txt))
- "Your first task is to locate and destroy each enemy flag tower to reveal the one containing your opponent's flag." "There is only one flag hidden in one of your enemy's towers. There can be many towers, so destroy each one until you find the flag. Be careful, the towers can be protected heavily by turrets." (same, "Your Mission" and "The Flag Tower")
- Modes: "One Player: Training ... The one player mode is a race against the clock and allows you to practice and hone your skills." "Two Player: Tournament — Two Players battle on a split screen as they destroy each others fortifications and vehicles. The first player to capture his opponent's flag and return to his own bunker wins the battle. Player 1 plays as the Brown squad and Player 2 as the Green squad." (same, "Main Menu")

### The flag rule
- "The 4th vehicle, the Humvee, is the only vehicle that can transport the flag." "To pick up a flag, just touch the flag with the Humvee." "If your Humvee is destroyed while you're carrying the flag, the flag remains at the location where you were destroyed and you must start again from your bunker with a new Humvee." "The only vehicle that can carry a flag (your flag or the opponent's flag)." ([Saturn manual, "Your Mission" and "Humvee"](https://archive.org/download/Return_Fire_1997_Silent_Software_US/Return_Fire_1997_Silent_Software_US_djvu.txt)). On 3DO/PC the vehicle is called the jeep: "the jeep is the fastest of the vehicles -- quick in and quick out ... The jeep has no armor. One hit, and it's history." ([Windows demo help file RFIRE.HLP, dated 9/5/95, in the 1996 demo](https://archive.org/details/Rfire))
- Defensive flag play is explicitly a mechanic: "If your enemy finds your flag, take your jeep and go retrieve your own flag. Then: 1. Hide it behind a building or turret 2. Move it to the farthest location on the map 3. Bring it back to your own bunker (it will randomly be placed in a new flag tower) - or - 4. You can take it out to sea, and it will slowly float back to shore." ([RFIRE.HLP, "Two Player Hints"](https://archive.org/details/Rfire))

### The four vehicles (numbers from the Saturn manual; "This amount varies based on the selected map's difficulty")
| Vehicle | Max qty | Ammo | Turret hits to die | Shots to kill a turret | Unique traits |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Helicopter | 3 | 96 shells, 48 missiles (two switchable weapon systems) | 11 | 2 | "Flies straight to the target"; "The only vehicle that can destroy mines"; "Uses gas quickly"; "Cannot refuel or rearm unless you return to the bunker"; radar has eight hash marks that light toward the enemy |
| Tank | 3 | 92 shells | 19 | 5 | "Travels faster than an ASV / Fires faster than an ASV / The turret can be rotated 360° to continue shooting at a target while you are moving" |
| ASV (Armoured Support Vehicle) | 3 | 64 missiles | 26 | 3 | "The only vehicle that can lay mines" (2-player only; "The mines gauge always reads empty in one player mode"); slower, fires slower, uses more gas than the tank |
| Humvee / Jeep | 3 | 16 grenades | 1 ("No armour. (One shot will destroy the Humvee)") | 5 grenades | Only flag carrier; fastest; "This vehicle can drive over deep water by inflating its tyres"; no radar, but a directional beacon (blue toward your bunker, green + chime toward the exposed enemy flag); grenades "effective for clearing the landscape of trees and brush or clearing debris from around the exposed flag" |

All rows: [Saturn manual, "The Vehicles"](https://archive.org/download/Return_Fire_1997_Silent_Software_US/Return_Fire_1997_Silent_Software_US_djvu.txt); the same role descriptions appear in the [Windows demo help](https://archive.org/details/Rfire) (which says the helicopter destroys mines "by firing rockets (and only rockets) directly at the mines" and that the jeep's dashboard light is red toward the bunker, green toward the flag).

### Bases, storage, respawn, repair
- "Inside your underground bunker are up to four vehicles to choose from ... The vehicle will move onto the elevator and rise out of the bunker." "As you play Return Fire, you may lose all the vehicles of a certain type. If so, the slot where that vehicle would have been displayed will be empty. Some maps actually start without certain vehicles." ([Saturn manual, "Vehicle Selection"](https://archive.org/download/Return_Fire_1997_Silent_Software_US/Return_Fire_1997_Silent_Software_US_djvu.txt))
- "Return to your bunker to select another vehicle, repair and reload, or deliver the enemy's flag and celebrate your victory." To enter, "drive or fly your vehicle directly over the top of the Bunker. When the Bunker's doors start to illuminate, press the A Button" (same, "The Bunker", "Returning to the Bunker"). "The bunker will repair all damage." ([RFIRE.HLP](https://archive.org/details/Rfire))
- A vehicle is a consumable life: there is no respawn other than taking the next vehicle of that type from the bunker (implicit in the max-quantity rules above). Self-destruct exists on PC: "You can self-destruct by pressing all control keys ... You will sacrifice the vehicle, but it's a quick way to get back to your bunker." ([RFIRE.HLP](https://archive.org/details/Rfire))

### Fuel, ammo, damage
- "When your Tank, ASV, or Humvee is low on fuel or ammo, pull up next to the depots, and they will be replenished. Your helicopter must return to your bunker to refuel and replenish ammo. Don't sit still too long while refueling or reloading. Enemy drones will find you on their RADAR and attack." ([Saturn manual, "Fuel and Ammo Depots"](https://archive.org/download/Return_Fire_1997_Silent_Software_US/Return_Fire_1997_Silent_Software_US_djvu.txt))
- Damage feedback: "As you take direct hits, a red (turning to yellow) filled circle will begin to spread outward on the RADAR screen. The larger the circle, the closer you are to destruction." (same, "Damage Control")
- Out-of-fuel behaviour (land vehicle stops, driver flees) is described only in a secondary source ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Return_Fire)).

### Map knowledge, radar, boundaries
- "An entire map of the war zone is available from the bunker ... you can only see the full map while in the bunker. The Helicopter, Tank and ASV each have RADAR installed on their consoles. The RADAR screens will only show information about your immediate area." The map shows island outlines, both bunkers (in Tournament), turret positions, walls, all flag towers and exposed flags ([Saturn manual, "Land Navigation"](https://archive.org/download/Return_Fire_1997_Silent_Software_US/Return_Fire_1997_Silent_Software_US_djvu.txt)).
- "Special provisions have been made for deserters. Do not leave the bounds of your assigned sector!" (same). The PC help explains the mechanism: "The submarine is deadly and cannot be destroyed. Once you have a heat-seeker on your tail, you're toast... unless something else is closer to the missile ... if a drone or even your enemy is closer to the missile, it will be destroyed instead." ([RFIRE.HLP](https://archive.org/details/Rfire))

### Destructible world
- Terrain changes are tracked: "Always check the map when you're in the bunker. It is constantly updated and will show you how the terrain has changed." Bridges can be destroyed or mined: "Mine the bridges which are useful to your enemy instead of destroying the bridges." ([RFIRE.HLP](https://archive.org/details/Rfire)). Grenades clear "trees and brush" ([Saturn manual](https://archive.org/download/Return_Fire_1997_Silent_Software_US/Return_Fire_1997_Silent_Software_US_djvu.txt)).
- Contemporary description of the result: "everything is up for being destroyed ... rubble and burning buildings"; "destroy your first building and watch its tiny inhabitants running for cover ... Fail to run them over and they'll start lobbing grenades at you"; the amphibious jeep is "vital ... when ... the bridge, connecting it to the mainland, has been destroyed" (secondary source: [3DO Magazine no. 2 review, pp. 51–53](https://archive.org/details/3-do-02)).

### Enemy behaviour / AI
- The designer's stated position (Feb 1995): "Return Fire does not have an intelligent, computer generated opponent because it was primarily designed for two players. I want people to play against each other ... using the computer as the 'board' on which to play the game." ([3DO Magazine no. 2, interview](https://archive.org/details/3-do-02)). Threats are turrets ("Turrets have the capability to destroy air, ground and floating targets"), drones that home on stationary vehicles, mines, and the boundary submarine ([Saturn manual](https://archive.org/download/Return_Fire_1997_Silent_Software_US/Return_Fire_1997_Silent_Software_US_djvu.txt); [RFIRE.HLP](https://archive.org/details/Rfire)). The single-player mode is explicitly framed as training against fortifications, not against an opposing commander (same).

### Maps, progression, saves
- Saturn/PlayStation-era structure: "The Level Selection Screen lets you choose from eighteen different difficulty levels. Each level consists of several maps of the battle terrain ... The red coloured levels may only be accessed using a password given following your last completed level"; with a memory card completed levels are saved automatically ([Saturn manual, "Map and Level Selection"](https://archive.org/download/Return_Fire_1997_Silent_Software_US/Return_Fire_1997_Silent_Software_US_djvu.txt)).
- Original 3DO structure: "The game is arranged with nine, progressively bigger sets of maps. To get to the next set of maps, all you have to do is complete one map on the previous set." (secondary source: [3DO Magazine no. 8](https://archive.org/details/3domagazineissue081996uk_202004)). **Unverified:** exact total map count of the original.
- Map editor: none is mentioned in the 3DO manual, the Saturn manual, or the PC help; I found no evidence of a level editor in any version, including Return Fire 2. **Unverified** only in the sense that absence of evidence is not proof; treat as "no editor".

### Camera, controls, rendering
- Steering options: "Vehicle Relative - This is the default setting ... Screen Relative - Pressing the Directional Buttons Up button moves the vehicle toward the top of the screen"; buttons for Zoom In / Zoom Out and "Toggle Screen/Fullscreen" (split vs full) ([Saturn manual, "The Bunker", "General Controls"](https://archive.org/download/Return_Fire_1997_Silent_Software_US/Return_Fire_1997_Silent_Software_US_djvu.txt)).
- The designer on the camera: "You get to view this world I'm showing you, you get to move forwards and backwards and you can zoom in, but you can't turn your head. So it's not real 3D, it's 2.5D, it is real 3D in the sense it's being rendered with a 3D formula, except, we dropped one of the axis and the reason we did this is because we gained about 20 percent more speed on the 3DO. And knowing we had to split the screen and render two worlds, we wanted to keep the frame rate north of 12 frames per second." ([Retro Gaming Blog interview, 14 May 2015](https://theretrogamingblog.wordpress.com/2015/05/14/reichart-von-wolfsheild-3do-return-fire-and-the-industry/))
- "It uses a lot of very tiny polygons, every vehicle, tree and so on is made up of polygons - everything. I estimate the 3DO is handling about 200,000 polygons per second ... it's got a transparent cockpit and you can see the men in there." ([3DO Magazine no. 2, interview](https://archive.org/details/3-do-02)). Dynamic zoom tied to speed ("The camera zooms in close as your tank rolls onto the tarmac, before smoothly pulling back") is a contemporary observation (secondary source: [3DO Magazine no. 2 review](https://archive.org/details/3-do-02)).

The score data on the disc carries cue markers "Wagner - Start 2 / Start 16 / Death / STOP", "Holst - Start 1 / 2 / 3 / Death / STOP", "Grieg - Start 1 / Death / STOP", "Rimsky - Start 1 / STOP", "Handel - Start 1 / STOP", "Rossini - Start 1 / Death / STOP", plus "Bunker Loop", "Death" and "Sub" cues, and the program's event list includes SCORE_TANK_1..3, SCORE_JEEP_1, SCORE_MSV_1, SCORE_HELI_1..2, SCORE_FLAG_DISCOVERY, SCORE_FLAG_PICKUP, SCORE_WIN, SCORE_BUNKER, SCORE_DEATH, SCORE_SUB (same disc image) — i.e. the music is event-driven with per-vehicle start points and death stingers. A contemporary reviewer described the effect: "The way the music changes according to what's happening on screen will have even hardened rockers humming along to Ride Of The Valkyries" (secondary source: [3DO Magazine no. 8](https://archive.org/details/3domagazineissue081996uk_202004)).

The developer on how the music was chosen and licensed: he went "through 300 classical CD's" over three months; "What I was looking for was the Rock 'n' Roll version of these songs, I wanted to hear the upbeat versions, I wanted to hear heavy drums, it was a video-game I wanted it over-the-top." Silent Software asked EMI for 45 minutes of music, EMI quoted $100,000, and "He finally agreed to full licensing rights for any games I make in the next three years ... part of the deal that actually pushed it through was I told them I would advertise them, I would put 'EMI' first image full page" ([Retro Gaming Blog interview, 14 May 2015](https://theretrogamingblog.wordpress.com/2015/05/14/reichart-von-wolfsheild-3do-return-fire-and-the-industry/)). The Apocalypse Now connection is also his: Francis Ford Coppola "played Return Fire at a party, and he laughed when he heard the music, of course – Ride of the Valkyries" (same). The victory-sequence claim ("and he shall reign forever and ever" over ticker-tape footage) I could only find in a secondary source ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Return_Fire)).

The laughing skull on death is RJ Mical's real laugh: "So that became the skull laughing. It was a way to add a little bit of levity" ([same interview](https://theretrogamingblog.wordpress.com/2015/05/14/reichart-von-wolfsheild-3do-return-fire-and-the-industry/)).

Design-relevant takeaway: the table above is the cue list to recreate. For a private build, use whichever recordings you like; the part worth copying is the structure: one theme per vehicle with alternative start points, stingers for flag discovery, pickup, death and the win, and a bunker loop. Recording licences only matter if the game is ever distributed; see the appendix.

## G. Design lessons in the developers' own words

Primary sources: the designer's February 1995 interview ([3DO Magazine no. 2, pp. 12–13](https://archive.org/details/3-do-02)), his May 2015 interview ([Retro Gaming Blog](https://theretrogamingblog.wordpress.com/2015/05/14/reichart-von-wolfsheild-3do-return-fire-and-the-industry/), republished with the same quotes on [LinkedIn, 30 Jan 2019](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/interview-reichart-von-wolfsheild-ashley-wing/)), and quotes in the accompanying review ([Retro Gaming Blog, 15 Jun 2015](https://theretrogamingblog.wordpress.com/2015/06/15/return-fire-review/)). I found no GDC talk or formal postmortem by the team. **Unverified:** existence of any developer talk or postmortem beyond these interviews.

- Build for two humans; the computer is the board. "I love multi-player games over single player games and I view the machine itself as nothing but a chessboard. It's backdrop." (2015). "I design computer games so that people can play against each other using the computer as the 'board' on which to play the game." (1995). Consequence: no AI commander; single-player is training and a clock.
- Equalise players through style, not stats. He wanted the fencing property where a smaller opponent wins on technique; asymmetric vehicles with hard trade-offs (fragile fast flag carrier vs. slow heavy ASV) create that space (2015 interview, "Roll Out"/"Genie" sections).
- Simplicity is the retention mechanic. Asked why people still love it: "Simplicity." Advice for a remake: "Keep our original simplicity, but kick up the realism, more detail, etc." (2015). "We wanted to build an arcade game. This was always my goal." (2015). "Return Fire was built with the care and consideration of the old days. You might say that it's an old fashioned game... destroy, destroy, destroy." (1995).
- Spend the frame budget on the world, and constrain the camera to pay for it. Fixed heading, zoom-only 2.5D bought ~20% and kept split-screen above 12 fps; "I had a lot to render, more than an EA game would do" (2015). "It took us about three months just to develop the 'look.'" (1995).
- Prototype physically. The team held a box slip-cover to their faces as a viewport over a table of die-cast toys to agree on what the camera could and could not do (2015).
- Design for the worst machine first. "I like to believe you should use the worst example of a machine a game could run on. If you can make the game fun on a primitive piece of technology, then you should be able to make that game fun on a more sophisticated piece of technology." (1995).
- Lineage and inspiration. Fire Power (the Amiga predecessor) was "a better version of Tank" and "very much modeled after Will Wright's Raid on Bungeling Bay", which he loved but "hated the fact it was one player" (2015).
- Music as character, deliberately over the top. Chose the most "Rock 'n' Roll" recordings with "heavy drums"; each vehicle and each key event got its own theme (see section C); licensed the masters and gave the label a full-page credit in exchange for price (2015).
- Keep the tone light. Real belly-laugh for the death skull "to add a little bit of levity to something that was taking on ... a rather dark mood"; "The tanks in Return Fire may squash people, but we hope we've done that in the most delightful way possible!" (2015; 1995). Relevant for a wasteland re-theme: the original's violence reads as toy-soldier slapstick, not grim.
- Small team, long back-burner. "Return Fire was a backburner project, just something we did in our spare time"; "Return took about two years in all" (1995); art by one fine artist taught to use a mouse (Van Arno), one programmer (Will Ware), one producer/sound designer (Alex Kasperavicius) plus the designer (1995, 2015).
- Expansion lesson (secondary source): the mission disc kept the graphics, cut vehicle supplies to force tactics, and added playful set-piece maps (an "S.O.S." map, a mine-covered island with two jeeps) — "a sense of playfulness that was lacking in the rather earnest original" ([3DO Magazine no. 8](https://archive.org/details/3domagazineissue081996uk_202004)).

### A.1 Split-screen local multiplayer

**Godot.** A `SubViewportContainer` is "a container that displays the contents of underlying SubViewport child nodes" ([SubViewportContainer](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_subviewportcontainer.html)); with `stretch` enabled "the sub-viewport will be automatically resized to the control's size" (same page). A `SubViewport` is "an interface to a game world that doesn't create a window or draw to the screen directly" ([SubViewport](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_subviewport.html)). Cameras attach to viewports automatically: "When using a Camera3D or Camera2D, it will always display on the closest parent Viewport (going towards the root)", and sub-viewports share the parent's 3D world unless you set `Own World3D` ([Viewports tutorial](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/rendering/viewports.html)). So a two-player screen is two `SubViewportContainer`s side by side (e.g. inside an `HBoxContainer`), each with a `SubViewport` and a `Camera3D`, both looking at the same world. The official demo repository contains a two-player "dynamic split screen" (Voronoi) implementation built from "two separate SubViewports, each containing a camera for one player", composited with a shader ([godot-demo-projects: dynamic_split_screen](https://github.com/godotengine/godot-demo-projects/tree/master/viewport/dynamic_split_screen)); a plain fixed split is simpler than that demo. For the overhead view, `Camera3D.projection` offers perspective, orthogonal ("Objects remain the same size on the screen no matter how far away they are") and frustum modes, and `make_current()` selects the active camera per viewport ([Camera3D](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_camera3d.html)).

**Unity.** `Camera.rect` is "where on the screen is the camera rendered in normalized coordinates", with (0,0) bottom-left and (1,1) top-right ([Camera.rect](https://docs.unity3d.com/ScriptReference/Camera-rect.html)); the manual states "you can set the size of a camera's onscreen rectangle using its Viewport Rect property" and that "a camera with a higher depth value is rendered over one with a lower value" ([Using more than one camera](https://docs.unity3d.com/Manual/MultipleCameras.html)). The Input System's `PlayerInputManager` component can "automatically split the available screen space between the active players", optionally into a fixed number of rectangles ([PlayerInputManager, Input System 1.14.2](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.inputsystem@1.14/manual/PlayerInputManager.html)).

**Unreal.** `UGameMapsSettings::bUseSplitscreen` is "whether the screen should be split or not when multiple local players are present", with `TwoPlayerSplitscreenLayout` etc. selecting the layout ([UGameMapsSettings](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/API/Runtime/EngineSettings/UGameMapsSettings)). Each active player has a `ULocalPlayer`: "there may be several spawned in the case of splitscreen/coop" ([ULocalPlayer](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/API/Runtime/Engine/ULocalPlayer?lang=en-US)). `GameplayStatics.create_player(world_context_object, controller_id=-1, spawn_player_controller=True)` will "Create a new local player for this game, for cases like local multiplayer", where controller_id is "The ID of the physical controller that the should control the newly created player. A value of -1 specifies to use the next available ID" ([Python API reference for GameplayStatics](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/python-api/class/GameplayStatics?application_version=5.3); the C++ page [UGameplayStatics::CreatePlayer](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/API/Runtime/Engine/Kismet/UGameplayStatics/CreatePlayer) is rendered client-side and could not be read by my tooling). For testing, the console command `debugCreatePlayer 1` adds a second local player and `ssswapControllers` switches controllers between players ([Split Screen, UE 4.27 docs](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/split-screen?application_version=4.27) — the page exists only in the 4.27 documentation tree). **Unverified:** the `UGameViewportClient` split-screen layout API description; its documentation page also rendered empty for me ([UGameViewportClient](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/API/Runtime/Engine/Engine/UGameViewportClient)).

**Bevy.** `Viewport` "defines the area on the render target to which the camera renders its image", with `physical_position` (top-left origin) and `physical_size` ([Viewport, Bevy 0.19.1](https://docs.rs/bevy/latest/bevy/camera/struct.Viewport.html)). The official example "Renders four cameras to the same window to accomplish 'split screen'", giving each camera a distinct `Camera { order: ... }` and recomputing viewports on `WindowResized` events ([examples/3d/split_screen.rs](https://github.com/bevyengine/bevy/blob/main/examples/3d/split_screen.rs); listed in the [examples README](https://github.com/bevyengine/bevy/blob/main/examples/README.md)).

### A.2 Routing multiple local gamepads to specific players

**Godot.** Every input event carries `device`, "The event's device ID" ([InputEvent](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_inputevent.html)). The `Input` singleton exposes `get_connected_joypads()` ("device IDs of all currently connected joypads"), the `joy_connection_changed` signal ("Emitted when a joypad device has been connected or disconnected"), `get_joy_axis(device, axis)`, `is_joy_button_pressed(device, button)` and `get_joy_name(device)`; controller names and mappings come from the SDL controller database ([Input](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_input.html)). The recommended pattern is input *actions* defined in Project Settings > Input Map rather than hard-coded buttons; the default deadzone is 0.5 and `Input.get_vector()` takes a deadzone as an optional 5th parameter ([Controllers, gamepads, and joysticks](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/inputs/controllers_gamepads_joysticks.html)). Actions are "a grouping of zero or more InputEvents into a commonly understood title", and `InputMap` "is ideal for reassigning or creating different actions at runtime" ([Using InputEvent](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/inputs/inputevent.html)). Practical approach for two players: define `p1_*` and `p2_*` actions, or one action set and filter by `event.device` in `_unhandled_input`. **Unverified:** whether the Input Map editor UI lets you pin an action's joypad event to a specific device index ("All Devices" vs "Device 0/1"); I could not find that statement in the fetched pages.

**Unity.** "Each `PlayerInput` can have one or more Devices assigned to it. By default, no two `PlayerInput` components are assigned the same Devices" ([PlayerInput](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.inputsystem@1.14/manual/PlayerInput.html)). `PlayerInputManager` joins players by listening "for button presses on Devices that are not paired to any player" and can limit the player count ([PlayerInputManager](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.inputsystem@1.14/manual/PlayerInputManager.html)). Under the hood "each InputUser represents a human interacting with the application", paired via `InputUser.PerformPairingWithDevice`, explicitly for "multiple users playing a game together on a single computer or device (local multiplayer)" ([User management](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.inputsystem@1.14/manual/UserManagement.html)). Version note: the Input System changelog lists 1.20.1 (2026-08-21) as the newest 1.x release and 6.7.0 (2026-09-08) as compatible with the Unity 6000.7.0a6 alpha, so on 6.3 LTS/6.6 you will use the 1.x line ([changelog](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.inputsystem@6.7/changelog/CHANGELOG.html)).

**Unreal.** "A PlayerController is the interface between the Pawn and the human player controlling it"; handling input in the PlayerController is needed for "multiple players on one game client" ([Player Controllers](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/player-controllers-in-unreal-engine)). Enhanced Input binds Input Mapping Contexts per player through the Enhanced Input Local Player Subsystem (`AddMappingContext()` with priorities) ([Enhanced Input](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/enhanced-input-in-unreal-engine)). Gamepad-to-player assignment is configured by `bOffsetPlayerGamepadIds` ("gamepads start being assigned to the second controller ID in local multiplayer games") and the "Skip Assigning Gamepad to Player 1" option ([UGameMapsSettings](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/API/Runtime/EngineSettings/UGameMapsSettings)); `ULocalPlayer::SetControllerId` changes "the physical ControllerId for this player" ([ULocalPlayer](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/API/Runtime/Engine/ULocalPlayer?lang=en-US)).

**Bevy.** Each connected gamepad is an entity: the `Gamepad` component "Stores a connected gamepad's metadata such as the name and its GamepadButton and GamepadAxis", and "An entity with this component is spawned automatically after GamepadConnectionEvent" ([Gamepad](https://docs.rs/bevy/latest/bevy/input/gamepad/struct.Gamepad.html)). The official example iterates `Query<(Entity, &Gamepad)>` and reads `gamepad.just_pressed(GamepadButton::South)` and `gamepad.get(GamepadAxis::LeftStickX)` ([examples/input/gamepad_input.rs](https://github.com/bevyengine/bevy/blob/main/examples/input/gamepad_input.rs)). Mapping player N to gamepad entity N is your own small system.

### A.3 Vehicle movement: physics vehicles versus arcade/kinematic movement

**Godot.** `VehicleBody3D` is "A 3D physics body that simulates the behavior of a car" (a RigidBody3D with `engine_force`, `brake`, `steering`) but the docs warn: "This class has known issues and isn't designed to provide realistic 3D vehicle physics", recommending custom physics on CharacterBody3D or RigidBody3D for advanced needs ([VehicleBody3D](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_vehiclebody3d.html); [VehicleWheel3D](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_vehiclewheel3d.html)). `CharacterBody3D` bodies "are meant to be user-controlled. They are not affected by physics at all, but they affect other physics bodies in their path", moved with `move_and_slide()`; `MOTION_MODE_FLOATING` is for games "without gravity concepts" where all collisions count as walls ([CharacterBody3D](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_characterbody3d.html)). `RigidBody3D` "cannot be controlled directly, instead, you must apply forces to it", and "Changing the 3D transform or linear_velocity of a RigidBody3D very often may lead to some unpredictable behaviors" ([RigidBody3D](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_rigidbody3d.html)). Jolt Physics "was added as an alternative to the existing Godot Physics physics engine in 4.4" and is the default 3D engine for new projects ([Using Jolt Physics](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/physics/using_jolt_physics.html)). Official demos: [Truck Town](https://github.com/godotengine/godot-demo-projects/tree/master/3d/truck_town) ("different types of trucks of varying complexity using vehicle physics", including a VehicleBody3D truck and joint-based trailers) and [Kinematic Character 3D](https://github.com/godotengine/godot-demo-projects/tree/master/3d/kinematic_character) (CharacterBody3D moved by `move_and_slide()` in `_physics_process()`).

**Unity.** WheelCollider "simulates a spring and damper suspension setup, and uses a slip based tire friction model to calculate wheel contact forces", driven by `motorTorque`, `steerAngle`, `brakeTorque` ([WheelCollider API](https://docs.unity3d.com/ScriptReference/WheelCollider.html); [manual](https://docs.unity3d.com/Manual/class-WheelCollider.html)). The arcade alternatives: CharacterController "is mainly used for third-person or first-person player control that does not make use of Rigidbody physics" and "does not react to forces on its own" ([Character Controller](https://docs.unity3d.com/Manual/class-CharacterController.html)); or a kinematic Rigidbody, where "the physics system cannot apply forces to move or rotate the GameObject" ([Rigidbody](https://docs.unity3d.com/Manual/class-Rigidbody.html)) moved with `Rigidbody.MovePosition` in `FixedUpdate` ([MovePosition](https://docs.unity3d.com/ScriptReference/Rigidbody.MovePosition.html)).

**Unreal.** Chaos Vehicles require a skeletal mesh, physics asset, animation blueprint, vehicle blueprint (from `WheeledVehiclePawn`), wheel blueprints (from `ChaosVehicleWheel`) and an engine torque curve, with the ChaosVehicles plugin enabled ([How to set up vehicles](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/how-to-set-up-vehicles-in-unreal-engine); overview at [Vehicles](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/vehicles-in-unreal-engine)). The arcade alternative is a `Pawn` ("the base class of all Actors that can be controlled by players or AI"); `DefaultPawn` ships a flying-style movement component with `MaxSpeed`, `Acceleration`, `Deceleration` and no gravity ([Pawn](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/pawn-in-unreal-engine)), and `FloatingPawnMovement` "provides simple movement for any Pawn class. Limits on speed and acceleration are provided, while gravity is not implemented" ([FloatingPawnMovement](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/python-api/class/FloatingPawnMovement?application_version=5.3)).

**Bevy.** Bevy has no built-in physics. Avian is "An ECS-driven 2D and 3D physics engine for the Bevy game engine" with dynamic, kinematic and static bodies; Avian 0.7 targets Bevy 0.19 ([Avian README](https://github.com/Jondolf/avian)); it ships `kinematic_character_3d`, `dynamic_character_3d` and `move_and_slide_3d` examples ([avian3d examples](https://github.com/Jondolf/avian/tree/main/crates/avian3d/examples)). The `bevy_rapier` repository is archived and the plugin now lives in the Rapier monorepo ([archived repo](https://github.com/dimforge/bevy_rapier), [new location](https://github.com/dimforge/rapier/tree/master/bindings/bevy_rapier)); Rapier's `KinematicCharacterController` implements "move-and-slide" for kinematic bodies ([Rapier docs](https://rapier.rs/docs/user_guides/bevy_plugin/character_controller)). **Unverified:** the current Rapier-Bevy version compatibility table (the README did not load).

**Recommendation for this game: kinematic/arcade movement, not physics vehicles.** Reasons grounded in the docs above: (1) Return Fire's vehicles behave like arcade units seen from above, and every engine documents its physics-vehicle path as a suspension-and-tyre simulation (Godot raycast wheels, Unity slip-based tyre model, Unreal skeletal mesh + torque curve) — far more setup than the design needs; (2) Godot's own reference says VehicleBody3D "has known issues", and its recommended alternative for custom behaviour is CharacterBody3D/RigidBody3D; (3) kinematic bodies give deterministic, tunable speed/turn rates and still collide (Godot `move_and_slide()`, Unity `MovePosition`, Unreal `FloatingPawnMovement`, Avian/Rapier move-and-slide), which is what "feels like Return Fire" requires. Use rigid bodies only for debris and knocked-over props. **Helicopter:** it needs its own simple flight model (altitude as a separate value, forward/strafe velocity with acceleration and drag, banking purely visual). In Godot, `CharacterBody3D` in `MOTION_MODE_FLOATING` is the natural fit because it is documented for gravity-less movement where "all collisions register as walls" ([CharacterBody3D](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_characterbody3d.html)); the same logic maps to `FloatingPawnMovement` in Unreal and a kinematic Rigidbody in Unity.

### A.4 Grid or tile-based terrain and maps

**Godot.** "Gridmaps are a tool for creating 3D game levels, similar to the way TileMap works in 2D", painted from a `MeshLibrary` in the editor; library items can carry collision shapes and navigation meshes ([Using GridMaps](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/3d/using_gridmaps.html)). The `GridMap` node "lets you place meshes on a grid interactively. It works both from the editor and from scripts"; `set_cell_item()` "Sets the mesh index for the cell referenced by its grid coordinates" with an orientation, `cell_size` defaults to (2,2,2), and `bake_navigation` creates navigation regions for cells whose item has a navigation mesh ([GridMap](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_gridmap.html)). That is exactly a Return Fire style map: a text or JSON file of cell codes fed to `set_cell_item()` at load time, or maps painted in the editor. For data files, `JSON.parse_string()`/`JSON.stringify()` ([JSON](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_json.html)) with `FileAccess.open("user://...")` as shown in [Saving games](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/io/saving_games.html); or custom `Resource` classes with `@export` fields saved as text `.tres` files, which Godot documents as version-control friendly and converts to binary on export ([Resources](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/scripting/resources.html)). For 2D minimaps or a 2D editor, `TileMapLayer` "uses a TileSet which contain a list of tiles which are used to create grid-based maps" (the older `TileMap` node is deprecated) ([TileMapLayer](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_tilemaplayer.html)). For grey-boxing, CSG nodes are "mainly intended for prototyping" ([CSG tools](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/3d/csg_tools.html)).

**Unity.** The Tilemap component is part of Unity's 2D toolset, with orientations XY, XZ, etc. ([Tilemap reference](https://docs.unity3d.com/Manual/tilemaps/work-with-tilemaps/tilemap-reference.html)); Unity's Terrain is a heightmap system ("adjust the height or appearance of your landscape, and add trees or grass") ([Terrain](https://docs.unity3d.com/Manual/script-Terrain.html)). A 3D tile map is therefore hand-rolled: prefabs instantiated on a grid — the manual's own example is "instantiate several copies of a block prefab to construct a wall" ([Instantiating Prefabs](https://docs.unity3d.com/Manual/instantiating-prefabs.html)) — with editor grid snapping for manual placement ([Grid snapping](https://docs.unity3d.com/Manual/GridSnapping.html)). **Unverified:** I found no built-in 3D grid-map/mesh-tile node in the Unity manual.

**Unreal.** Landscape is "a collection of tools that allow you to create expansive outdoor environments" (sculpt and paint) ([Landscape](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/landscape-outdoor-terrain-in-unreal-engine)); grid tiles would be Actors spawned with `UWorld::SpawnActor()` from map data ([Spawning Actors](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/spawning-actors-in-unreal-engine)).

**Bevy.** `bevy_ecs_tilemap` is a 2D "tilemap rendering plugin for bevy" with Tiled and LDtk loading examples, version 0.19 for Bevy 0.19, MIT ([repo](https://github.com/StarArawn/bevy_ecs_tilemap)). A 3D grid is entities spawned per cell from a glTF scene via `Commands` ([Commands](https://docs.rs/bevy/latest/bevy/ecs/system/struct.Commands.html)).

### A.5 Destructible objects

The basic approach is the same everywhere: a building is a scene/prefab/actor with hit points; on death, remove it and spawn a "wrecked" variant plus a few debris rigid bodies and an effect; for bridges, also update the map's passability.

- **Godot:** scenes are blueprints you instance repeatedly ("Replicating an object from a template like this is called instancing") ([Creating instances](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/getting_started/step_by_step/instancing.html)); debris are `RigidBody3D` nodes given `apply_impulse()` ([RigidBody3D](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_rigidbody3d.html)); hits and pickups are detected with `Area3D`, "A region of 3D space that detects other CollisionObject3Ds entering or exiting it", via `body_entered` ([Area3D](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_area3d.html)); GridMap cells can be cleared with `set_cell_item()` ([GridMap](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_gridmap.html)).
- **Unity:** prefabs "instantiate complicated GameObjects or collections of GameObjects at runtime", e.g. "exploding projectiles" ([Instantiating Prefabs](https://docs.unity3d.com/Manual/instantiating-prefabs.html)).
- **Unreal:** Chaos is "Unreal Engine's high-performance physics and destruction system" ([Chaos Destruction](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/chaos-destruction-in-unreal-engine)); Geometry Collections "can be built from one or more Static Meshes", fractured in Fracture Mode, and "The most common way to apply strain to a Geometry Collection is by using a Physics Field" ([Destruction Quick Start](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/destruction-quick-start)). This is far more than a low-poly arcade game needs, but it exists.
- **Bevy:** `Commands` is "A Command queue to perform structural changes to the World" (spawn/despawn applied at sync points) ([Commands](https://docs.rs/bevy/latest/bevy/ecs/system/struct.Commands.html)).

### A.6 Simple AI for turrets and units

Turrets need only "acquire nearest enemy in range, rotate, fire with cooldown" — a hand-written finite state machine (see C.4). Patrol units need pathfinding:

- **Godot:** NavigationServer3D finds "the shortest path between two positions on an area defined by a navigation mesh"; `NavigationRegion3D` holds the mesh, `NavigationAgent3D` "is a helper Node used to facilitate common NavigationServer3D API calls for pathfinding and avoidance", `NavigationObstacle3D` constrains avoidance, `NavigationLink3D` connects regions ([3D navigation overview](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/navigation/navigation_introduction_3d.html)). With `NavigationAgent3D`, `get_next_path_position()` "must be used once every physics frame", and RVO avoidance is computed before physics ([NavigationAgent3D](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_navigationagent3d.html)). GridMap items can bake navigation per cell ([GridMap](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_gridmap.html)). Godot's built-in state machine is for animation only (`AnimationNodeStateMachine`, with `travel()`) ([AnimationTree](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/animation/animation_tree.html)); gameplay FSMs are plain code. Groups ("Groups in Godot work like tags") let you broadcast to all turrets with `call_group()` ([Groups](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/scripting/groups.html)). Official demo: [3D navigation](https://github.com/godotengine/godot-demo-projects/tree/master/3d/navigation).
- **Unity:** the AI Navigation package (2.0.15) provides NavMesh Surface, Agent, Obstacle, Off-Mesh Link and runtime baking ([AI Navigation](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.ai.navigation@2.0/manual/index.html)); Unity Behavior (1.0.16) is "a visual tool for authoring behaviors" using behavior graphs ([Unity Behavior](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.behavior@1.0/manual/index.html)).
- **Unreal:** the Navigation System "generates a Navigation Mesh from the collision geometry in the Level" ([Navigation System](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/navigation-system-in-unreal-engine)); Behavior Trees with a Blackboard drive NPC logic ([Behavior Trees](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/behavior-trees-in-unreal-engine)); StateTree "is a general-purpose hierarchical state machine that combines the Selectors from behavior trees with States and Transitions from state machines" ([StateTree](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/state-tree-in-unreal-engine)).
- **Bevy:** `vleue_navigator` is a "Navigation mesh for Bevy using Polyanya" (0.16 for Bevy 0.19, MIT/Apache-2.0) ([repo](https://github.com/vleue/vleue_navigator)); Bevy's `States` are "app-wide interdependent, finite state machines" with `OnEnter`/`OnExit` schedules — for menu/game-over flow, not per-unit AI ([bevy::state](https://docs.rs/bevy/latest/bevy/state/index.html)). The `big-brain` utility-AI crate is archived on GitHub (moved to Codeberg) and last targeted Bevy 0.16 ([repo](https://github.com/zkat/big-brain)).

### A.7 Scripting languages, learning curve, macOS editor, export targets

**Godot.** "Godot offers four gameplay programming languages: GDScript, C#, and, via its GDExtension technology, C and C++"; "If you're a beginner, we recommend to start with GDScript" ([Scripting languages](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/getting_started/step_by_step/scripting_languages.html)). GDScript is "a high-level, object-oriented, imperative, and gradually typed programming language built for Godot" with "an indentation-based syntax similar to languages like Python" ([GDScript reference](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/scripting/gdscript/gdscript_basics.html)). C# "projects written in C# support all desktop platforms (Windows, Linux, and macOS), as well as Android and iOS", but "cannot be exported to the web platform" ([C#](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/scripting/c_sharp/index.html)). macOS editor: x86_64 or Apple silicon, macOS 10.15+ for the Forward+/Mobile renderers, 4 GB RAM minimum / 8 GB recommended; exported games need macOS 13.0 for Metal ([System requirements](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/about/system_requirements.html)); the download is one universal binary ([download](https://godotengine.org/download/macos/)). Exports: Windows, macOS, Linux, Android, iOS, Web via installable export templates ([Exporting projects](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/export/exporting_projects.html)); Web needs WebAssembly and WebGL 2.0 and is single-threaded by default since 4.3 ([Exporting for the Web](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/export/exporting_for_web.html)). Consoles: only Steam Deck is officially supported; "The reasons other consoles are not officially supported are the risks of legal liability, disproportionate cost, and open source licensing issues", and a table of porting companies (e.g. W4 Games middleware for Switch/Xbox Series/PS5) is given ([Console support, Godot 4.4 docs](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.4/tutorials/platform/consoles.html) — **Unverified:** this page returned 404 at the same path in the 4.7 "stable" docs; I could not locate its new location).

**Unity.** C# throughout; the beginner path is the free "Unity Essentials" pathway, "designed for anyone new to Unity", about 2 weeks ([Unity Learn](https://learn.unity.com/pathway/unity-essentials)). macOS editor: "Ventura 13 or newer", "Apple M1 or above", Intel x64 "(deprecated)", Metal GPUs ([System requirements, Unity 6.6](https://docs.unity3d.com/Manual/system-requirements.html)). Platforms: Windows, macOS, Linux, Android, iOS, tvOS, visionOS, Web, Dedicated Server, embedded; PlayStation, Xbox and Nintendo Switch are closed platforms that "require confidentiality and legal agreements with the platform provider" ([Platform development](https://docs.unity3d.com/Manual/PlatformSpecific.html)).

**Unreal.** Blueprints are "a node-based interface to create gameplay elements from within Unreal Editor" and "a complete gameplay scripting system" layered on C++ ([Blueprints](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/blueprints-visual-scripting-in-unreal-engine)). macOS: minimum "Sonoma 14.5", recommended "Latest macOS Sequoia 15"; Xcode 26.0 minimum (26.1.1 recommended; "Xcode 26.4 is not compatible with Unreal Engine"); 16 GB RAM minimum, "32 GB or more" recommended; Apple silicon M1+ ([macOS development requirements, UE 5.8](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/macos-development-requirements-for-unreal-engine)). Platform guides cover Windows, iOS/iPadOS/tvOS, Android, XR and Pixel Streaming ([Sharing and releasing](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/sharing-and-releasing-projects-for-unreal-engine)); consoles (Switch, Switch 2, PS4, PS5, Xbox) require you to "register as a developer" with the platform holder ([Consoles development](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/consoles-development-in-unreal-engine)). Engine source is on GitHub for registered users ([forum announcement](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/unreal-engine-5-8-released/2729274)).

**Bevy.** Rust; no editor — "Bevy is still in the early stages of development. Important features are missing. Documentation is sparse", and "A new version of Bevy containing breaking changes to the API is released approximately once every 3 months" ([README](https://github.com/bevyengine/bevy/blob/main/README.md)). The 0.19 post describes the scene editor as still in development ([Bevy 0.19](https://bevy.org/news/bevy-0-19/)). Platforms: "Windows, MacOS, Linux, Web, iOS, Android" ([bevy.org](https://bevy.org/)); macOS setup requires the Xcode command-line tools, and the guide covers dynamic linking and linker choices to cut compile times ([Setup](https://bevy.org/learn/quick-start/getting-started/setup/)).

## B. Recommendation

**Primary choice: Godot 4.7 with GDScript.** Reasoning, tied to the facts above:

1. **Learning curve for a newcomer.** Godot's own docs say to start with GDScript, a Python-like language "made specifically for Godot and the needs of game developers" ([Scripting languages](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/getting_started/step_by_step/scripting_languages.html)), and the getting-started path ends in a complete 3D game tutorial built on the very node this project needs (CharacterBody3D) ([Your first 3D game](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/getting_started/first_3d_game/index.html)). Unreal's minimum macOS spec is 16 GB RAM with Xcode 26 and 32 GB recommended ([UE macOS requirements](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/macos-development-requirements-for-unreal-engine)); Godot's editor minimum is 4 GB and one universal binary ([Godot requirements](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/about/system_requirements.html)).
2. **Every building block this game needs is a built-in node with an official demo:** SubViewport split screen (demo), CharacterBody3D floating mode for arcade vehicles and the helicopter (demo), GridMap for grid-based maps authored in the editor or from data, NavigationAgent3D for units (demo), Area3D for flags and hit zones, JSON/Resource files for maps — all cited in A.1–A.6. Unity needs a hand-rolled 3D tile grid (A.4) and Bevy needs third-party crates for physics, navigation and tiles (A.3, A.6).
3. **Zero licence friction and Git-friendly project files.** MIT with attribution only ([licence](https://godotengine.org/license/)); text-based `.tscn/.tres` files that "Godot aims to be VCS-friendly" with, and a generated `.gitignore/.gitattributes` ([Version control systems](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/best_practices/version_control_systems.html)) — versus Unreal's checkout-based workflow because assets cannot be text-merged (D.4).
4. **Exports from macOS to Windows, macOS, Linux and Web** with universal macOS binaries ([Exporting for macOS](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/export/exporting_for_macos.html); [Windows](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/export/exporting_for_windows.html); [Linux](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/export/exporting_for_linux.html); [Web](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/export/exporting_for_web.html)).

**Fallback: Unity 6.3 LTS (C#, Input System, AI Navigation).** Choose it instead when any of these hold: (a) you already know C# well and want to stay in it while keeping a Web export (Godot's C# cannot export to Web — [C# docs](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/scripting/c_sharp/index.html)); (b) consoles are a real goal — Unity ships closed-platform modules once you have the platform agreements ([Platform development](https://docs.unity3d.com/Manual/PlatformSpecific.html)), whereas Godot relies on third-party porting companies ([Console support, 4.4 docs](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.4/tutorials/platform/consoles.html)); (c) you want the Input System's `PlayerInputManager` join-and-split-screen automation out of the box ([PlayerInputManager](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.inputsystem@1.14/manual/PlayerInputManager.html)). Cost is not a differentiator at hobby scale: Personal is free under USD 200k ([Pricing](https://unity.com/pricing)). The trade-offs: no built-in 3D grid map (A.4), and a commercial licence whose terms have changed several times since 2023 (A.8).

**When not to pick the others.** Unreal is the right tool for a photoreal or console-first production with a team; for a solo low-poly arcade remake its hardware floor, C++/Blueprint workflow, and binary assets are cost without benefit, and 5.8 is documented (Unverified, A.9) as the last UE5 release. Bevy is an excellent choice only if you specifically want to learn Rust and accept no editor, sparse docs and breaking releases roughly every three months ([README](https://github.com/bevyengine/bevy/blob/main/README.md)); the `physics_in_fixed_timestep` and `split_screen` examples show the fundamentals are there, but everything above them is assembled from crates.

## C. Core game-programming fundamentals to understand first

### C.1 The game loop and fixed versus variable timestep

Glenn Fiedler's essay is the canonical text: with a variable delta time "The behavior of your physics simulation depends on the delta time you pass in"; a semi-fixed step risks the "spiral of death"; the fix is an accumulator where "The renderer produces time and the simulation consumes it in discrete dt sized steps", plus interpolation between the previous and current state using the leftover fraction ([Fix Your Timestep!](https://gafferongames.com/post/fix_your_timestep/)). Robert Nystrom's Game Loop chapter states the intent — "Decouple the progression of game time from user input and processor speed" — and describes "fixed update time step, variable rendering" with a lag accumulator and passing the residual fraction to the renderer ([Game Loop](https://gameprogrammingpatterns.com/game-loop.html)).

How each engine exposes it:

- **Godot:** `_process()` runs "every frame, as often as possible" with a variable `delta`; `_physics_process()` "happens at a fixed rate, 60 times per second by default", configurable in Project Settings ([Idle and physics processing](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/scripting/idle_and_physics_processing.html)). Physics interpolation is "optional and disabled by default", enabled under Project Settings > Physics > Common > Physics Interpolation for both 2D and 3D; then "Move (almost) all game logic from `_process` to `_physics_process`" and call `reset_physics_interpolation()` after teleports ([Introduction](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/physics/interpolation/physics_interpolation_introduction.html), [Using physics interpolation](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/physics/interpolation/using_physics_interpolation.html)). Rule for this game: vehicle movement, firing, fuel/ammo and AI in `_physics_process`; camera smoothing and HUD in `_process`.
- **Unity:** `FixedUpdate` is "called at regular, fixed intervals as part of Unity's physics update loop" and "may be called zero, one, or multiple times per frame" ([MonoBehaviour.FixedUpdate](https://docs.unity3d.com/ScriptReference/MonoBehaviour.FixedUpdate.html)); `Update` "is called every frame" with `Time.deltaTime` ([MonoBehaviour.Update](https://docs.unity3d.com/ScriptReference/MonoBehaviour.Update.html)); the Fixed Timestep and Maximum Allowed Timestep live in the Time settings ([Time](https://docs.unity3d.com/Manual/class-TimeManager.html); overall order in [Execution order](https://docs.unity3d.com/Manual/execution-order.html)). `Rigidbody.interpolation` "calculates the pose of a Rigidbody in frames that fall between physics timestep updates, to reduce the appearance of visible jitter" ([Rigidbody.interpolation](https://docs.unity3d.com/ScriptReference/Rigidbody-interpolation.html)).
- **Unreal:** actors tick "typically once per frame" in tick groups (`TG_PrePhysics`, `TG_DuringPhysics`, `TG_PostPhysics`, `TG_PostUpdateWork`) ([Actor ticking](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/actor-ticking-in-unreal-engine)); because "Unreal Engine 5 uses a variable frame rate", physics sub-stepping "takes the total frame time and divides it into sub-steps" bounded by Max Substep Delta Time and Max Substeps ([Physics sub-stepping](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/physics-sub-stepping-in-unreal-engine)).
- **Bevy:** `FixedUpdate` is "The schedule that contains most gameplay logic, which runs at a fixed rate rather than every render frame" ([FixedUpdate](https://docs.rs/bevy/latest/bevy/app/struct.FixedUpdate.html)); `Time<Fixed>` defaults to 64 Hz and "the schedule may run 0, 1 or more times during a single update" ([Time<Fixed>](https://docs.rs/bevy/latest/bevy/time/struct.Fixed.html)). The official example advances physics in `FixedUpdate`, accumulates input before it and interpolates the rendered transform after it — Fiedler's pattern verbatim ([physics_in_fixed_timestep.rs](https://github.com/bevyengine/bevy/blob/main/examples/movement/physics_in_fixed_timestep.rs)).

### C.2 Update/render separation

The practical consequence of C.1: simulation state (positions, velocities, hit points, fuel) is authoritative and advances in fixed steps; what the player sees is derived from it each frame, interpolated where needed. Fiedler's "final touch" is exactly this ([Fix Your Timestep!](https://gafferongames.com/post/fix_your_timestep/)), and Godot's, Unity's and Bevy's interpolation facilities above implement it for you. Keep HUD, camera lag and particle effects out of the simulation step.

### C.3 Scene or entity composition

- **Godot:** nodes have a name, editable properties, per-frame callbacks and can be extended and parented; a scene is a tree of nodes with "one root node" that "you can save ... and load later" and instance "as many ... as you'd like" ([Nodes and scenes](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/getting_started/step_by_step/nodes_and_scenes.html)). A vehicle is a scene: `CharacterBody3D` root, `CollisionShape3D`, mesh, `Area3D` for pickups, a `Node3D` turret pivot, script on the root. Persistent game state (scores, match settings) goes in an autoload singleton, which exists because "there is no method for storing information (e.g. a player's score or inventory) that is needed by more than one scene" ([Singletons (Autoload)](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/scripting/singletons_autoload.html)).
- **Unity:** "GameObjects ... act as containers for Components, which implement the functionality", every GameObject has a Transform ([GameObjects](https://docs.unity3d.com/Manual/GameObjects.html)). Nystrom notes "The Unity framework's core GameObject class is designed entirely around components" ([Component](https://gameprogrammingpatterns.com/component.html)).
- **Unreal:** an "Actor is any object that can be placed into a level", actors "are containers that hold special types of Objects called Components", with a Root Component supplying the transform ([Actors](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/actors-in-unreal-engine)).
- **Bevy:** "ECS is a software pattern that involves breaking your program up into Entities, Components, and Systems"; entities are integers, components are Rust structs, systems are functions, run in parallel by default ([ECS](https://bevy.org/learn/quick-start/getting-started/ecs/)).

Nystrom's Component chapter gives the engine-independent intent: "Allow a single entity to span multiple domains without coupling the domains to each other" ([Component](https://gameprogrammingpatterns.com/component.html)).

### C.4 State machines

Nystrom's State chapter shows why boolean flags for character states collapse ("Every time we touch this handful of code, we break something") and defines an FSM: a fixed set of states, "The machine can only be in one state at a time", inputs, and per-state transitions; it then covers hierarchical state machines (shared "on ground" behaviour) and pushdown automata (a state stack for "fire, then return to whatever you were doing") ([State](https://gameprogrammingpatterns.com/state.html)). For this game: turret = Idle / Tracking / Firing / Cooldown / Destroyed; patrol unit = Patrol / Chase / Attack / ReturnToPath; match = Lobby / Countdown / Playing / FlagCaptured / RoundOver. Engine-level equivalents: Bevy `States` for the match flow ([bevy::state](https://docs.rs/bevy/latest/bevy/state/index.html)); Unreal StateTree ([StateTree](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/state-tree-in-unreal-engine)); Godot has none for gameplay (use an enum and a `match` statement).

### C.5 Input handling patterns

Nystrom's Command chapter: "A command is a reified method call"; mapping buttons to Command objects adds "a layer of indirection" that enables rebinding, and passing the actor into `execute(GameActor& actor)` means "AI engines and player input both produce commands", so the same vehicle code is driven by a gamepad or by AI ([Command](https://gameprogrammingpatterns.com/command.html)). This is the right shape for Return Fire: a `VehicleInput` struct (throttle, steer, fire, altitude) produced either by a gamepad reader or by an AI controller, consumed by the vehicle each fixed step. Godot's Input Map is the engine-level version of the rebinding layer (A.2).

### C.6 3D math primers

- Godot's Vector math tutorial is "a short and practical introduction to linear algebra as it applies to game development" — magnitude, normalisation, dot product ("one of the most important concepts in vector math"), cross product, reflection ([Vector math](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/math/vector_math.html)); the transforms tutorial covers `Transform3D`/`Basis`, that "all transform math is done relative to the parent node", and that "order matters" when composing ([Matrices and transforms](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/math/matrices_and_transforms.html)).
- Unity: vectors "describe a direction and magnitude"; the dot product shows "how far the first vector extends in the second's direction"; the cross product yields "a new vector that is perpendicular to the plane" ([Vectors](https://docs.unity3d.com/Manual/scripting-vectors.html)).
- Unreal: "the coordinate system is left-handed and uses a Z-up axis" — different from Godot and Unity ([Coordinate system and spaces](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/coordinate-system-and-spaces-in-unreal-engine)).
- Bevy: `Transform` holds translation, rotation, scale relative to the parent; `GlobalTransform` is propagated in `PostUpdate`, so writes after propagation lag a frame ([Transform](https://docs.rs/bevy/latest/bevy/transform/components/struct.Transform.html)).

What you actually need for an overhead arcade game: forward vector from a yaw angle, dot product for "is the target in front of the turret", distance checks for range and pickup radius, lerp for camera and turret rotation. The Godot tutorials cover all of it.

### D.2 Prototyping and the vertical slice

- The Experimental Gameplay Project team (Gabler, Gray, Kucic, Shodhan; article dated 26 Oct 2005) worked under three rules — each game in under seven days, by one person, on a theme — and distilled tips including "Enforce Short Development Cycles", "Build the Toy First", "If You Can Get Away With It, Fake It", "Cut Your Losses", "Heavy Theming Will Not Salvage Bad Design", "Nobody Cares About Your Great Engineering", "Complexity is Not Necessary for Fun" and "Make it Juicy" ([How to Prototype a Game in Under 7 Days](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/how-to-prototype-a-game-in-under-7-days); also a GDC talk, [GDC Vault](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1013294/How-to-Prototype-a-Game)). For you: the "toy" is one vehicle driving on a plane with a gamepad; if that is not fun to drive, nothing else matters.
- Rami Ismail: "Prototypes are to help you figure out whether you should make a game, and the Vertical Slice is to help you figure out whether you can make it"; prototypes should be "fast, cheap, and precise", using placeholder assets and cutting anything that does not answer the question ([Prototypes & Vertical Slice](https://ltpf.ramiismail.com/prototypes-and-vertical-slice/)).
- Greg Donovan (Volition, GDC 2015) describes the vertical slice as the gate that shows "whether or not the team is ready to move from pre-production into production" ([The Vertical Slice Challenge](https://gdcvault.com/play/1022328/The-Vertical-Slice)). Jonathan Blow's GDC 2007 talk on indie prototyping is listed but has no public abstract on the Vault page ([GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1014831/Indie-Prototyping-Braid-Making-Innovative)).
- For this project the vertical slice is milestone E.2: two players, split screen, one map, flags and scoring, with placeholder boxes — one complete round of the actual game.

### D.3 Scope control: finish small things first

Derek Yu's own writings on his site: the "death loops" that stop projects — the Loop of Restarting ("their skills would improve enough that they couldn't bear to finish the game with the work they had already done") and the Loop of Polishing ("It's also hard to know when to stop adding polish") — with the advice to scope from games you have actually finished and to save polish for the end ([Indie Game Dev: Death Loops](https://www.derekyu.com/makegames/deathloops.html)); "The threshold for success goes up the more resources you invest in your game", so "it makes sense for new developers to create games that are small BUT ALSO as big-feeling and fiddly as they can muster" ([Assessing Risk](https://www.derekyu.com/makegames/risk.html)). His earlier essay "Finishing a Game" is at [makegames.tumblr.com](https://makegames.tumblr.com/post/1136623767/finishing-a-game) — **Unverified:** that page sits behind a JavaScript check and could not be fetched, so I am not quoting its tips. Applied here: ship a two-player, one-map, two-vehicle version before adding the other vehicles, AI or a map editor.

### D.4 Version control for game projects

- **Git LFS** "replaces large files such as audio samples, videos, datasets, and graphics with text pointers inside Git, while storing the file contents on a remote server"; set up with `git lfs install`, `git lfs track "*.psd"`, then commit `.gitattributes` ([git-lfs.com](https://git-lfs.com/)). Track `.glb`, `.png`, `.wav`, `.ogg`, `.blend` from day one; retro-fitting LFS rewrites history.
- **Godot:** "Godot aims to be VCS-friendly and generate mostly readable and mergeable files"; the project manager generates `.gitignore` and `.gitattributes` (Project > Version Control > Generate Version Control Metadata for existing projects); ignore `.godot/` (cache); the docs give a Git LFS `.gitattributes` example covering models, images, audio and binary Godot resources; "As of October 2025, there is only a Git plugin available" for the editor ([Version control systems](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/best_practices/version_control_systems.html)).
- **Unity:** `.meta` files "contain the unique ID assigned to the asset, and values for all the asset's import settings" and must be committed with their assets ([Asset metadata](https://docs.unity3d.com/Manual/AssetMetadata.html)); "Visible Meta Files" is the mode for "a version control system that Unity doesn't support" and is the default ([Version control settings](https://docs.unity3d.com/Manual/class-VersionControlSettings.html)); the text (YAML) scene format exists because "text files generated separately can be merged more easily than binary files" ([Text-based scene format](https://docs.unity3d.com/Manual/TextSceneFormat.html)); `UnityYAMLMerge` merges "scene and prefab files in a semantically correct way" and can be wired into `.gitconfig` ([Smart merge](https://docs.unity3d.com/Manual/SmartMerge.html)). Exclude the `Library` folder ("just a local cache") and version `Assets` and `ProjectSettings` including `.meta` files — stated in the older Unity 4.3 manual ([Version control integration, Unity 4.3](https://docs.unity3d.com/430/Documentation/Manual/Versioncontrolintegration.html)); **Unverified:** I could not locate the equivalent page in the current (6.6) manual.
- **Unreal:** "Perforce and SVN are supported by default" in the editor ([Source control](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/source-control-in-unreal-engine)); checking out an asset "prevents other users from editing the asset at the same time" ([Source control, UE 4.27](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/source-control?application_version=4.27)); the collaboration overview lists Perforce, SVN, Anchorpoint, Lore ("a next-generation open-source version control system maintained by Epic Games") and Diversion ([Collaboration and version control](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/collaboration-and-version-control-in-unreal-engine)). **Unverified:** the often-quoted statement that `.uasset`/`.umap` files "are binary, so cannot be opened as text or merged in a text-based merge tool" appeared only in a search snippet of Epic's docs; I could not fetch the page containing it.

### D.5 Playtesting basics

Valve's GDC 2009 talk by Mike Ambinder ([GDC Vault](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1566/Valve-s-Approach-to-Playtesting)); the slides are hosted by Valve: "Game designs are hypotheses / Playtests are experiments / Evaluate designs off playtest results / Repeat"; "Get data early, get data often / Iterate constantly / We don't know what's best (players do)"; playtesting is for "Fun — Not bug testing — Not game balancing"; "Nothing beats direct gameplay observation"; note the "Importance of what people do — not what they say" and that observers can bias results ([Valve's Approach to Playtesting, slides PDF](https://cdn.akamai.steamstatic.com/apps/valve/2009/GDC2009_ValvesApproachToPlaytesting.pdf)). Derek Yu adds: "Players will know HOW they feel about your game, but they may not necessarily know WHY", and "If a large number of players are complaining about the same thing, you may want to prioritize making a change" ([Getting Feedback](https://www.derekyu.com/makegames/feedback.html)). For a two-player couch game this is easy and cheap: put a second person on the sofa at the end of every milestone, watch silently, write down what they did, not what they said.

One vehicle on a flat plane with one gamepad.** New project (Forward+ or Compatibility renderer), a `StaticBody3D` plane, a `CharacterBody3D` jeep with a box collider, `Camera3D` above it (start with perspective looking down; try orthogonal later). Input actions `throttle`, `steer`, `fire` mapped to a joypad axis/buttons; move in `_physics_process` with `move_and_slide()`; camera follow in `_process`. Docs: [CharacterBody3D](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_characterbody3d.html), [Idle and physics processing](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/scripting/idle_and_physics_processing.html), [Controllers, gamepads, and joysticks](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/inputs/controllers_gamepads_joysticks.html), [Camera3D](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_camera3d.html), [CSG tools](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/3d/csg_tools.html) for quick obstacles. Exit criterion: driving feels good to you for five minutes (Gabler's "Build the Toy First").

**1. Two players, split screen, two gamepads.** An `HBoxContainer` with two `SubViewportContainer`s (stretch on), each with a `SubViewport` and its own `Camera3D`; vehicles get a `device` index and read `Input.get_joy_axis(device, ...)`/`is_joy_button_pressed(device, ...)` or per-player actions; handle `joy_connection_changed`. Docs: [Viewports](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/rendering/viewports.html), [SubViewportContainer](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_subviewportcontainer.html), [SubViewport](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_subviewport.html), [Input](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_input.html), [InputEvent](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_inputevent.html), [dynamic_split_screen demo](https://github.com/godotengine/godot-demo-projects/tree/master/viewport/dynamic_split_screen). Also add physics interpolation now, while the project is small ([Using physics interpolation](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/physics/interpolation/using_physics_interpolation.html)).

**2. Flags, bases, capture, scoring (the vertical slice).** Flag = scene with `Area3D` + mesh; `body_entered` attaches it to the vehicle; base zone = `Area3D`; a `GameState` autoload keeps scores and the match FSM (C.4) and emits signals the HUD listens to; a `Label` per viewport. Docs: [Area3D](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_area3d.html), [Using signals](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/getting_started/step_by_step/signals.html), [Singletons (Autoload)](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/scripting/singletons_autoload.html), [Groups](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/scripting/groups.html), [Size and anchors](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/ui/size_and_anchors.html). Playtest with a second person here (D.5).

**3. Turrets and simple AI units.** Turret: `Node3D` pivot with an FSM (Idle/Tracking/Firing/Cooldown), target = nearest vehicle in a `"vehicles"` group within range, projectiles as `Area3D` or `RayCast3D` hits. Patrol units: `NavigationRegion3D` baked over the map, `NavigationAgent3D` on a `CharacterBody3D`, `get_next_path_position()` each physics frame; drive them through the same `VehicleInput` struct as players (C.5). Docs: [3D navigation overview](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/navigation/navigation_introduction_3d.html), [NavigationAgent3D](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_navigationagent3d.html), [3D navigation demo](https://github.com/godotengine/godot-demo-projects/tree/master/3d/navigation), Nystrom [State](https://gameprogrammingpatterns.com/state.html) and [Command](https://gameprogrammingpatterns.com/command.html).

**4. All four vehicle types with fuel and ammo.** One `Vehicle` base scene + script; per-type stats (speed, turn rate, armour, fuel capacity, ammo types, altitude for the helicopter) in a custom `Resource` (`VehicleStats.tres`) so tuning is data, not code. Helicopter: `MOTION_MODE_FLOATING`, altitude as a separate value, collision layers so it flies over ground units. Fuel drains per physics step; refuel/rearm zones are `Area3D`s at bases. Docs: [Resources](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/scripting/resources.html), [CharacterBody3D](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_characterbody3d.html) (motion modes), [Physics introduction](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/physics/physics_introduction.html) (collision layers and masks), [RigidBody3D](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_rigidbody3d.html) for knock-about props only.

**5. Grid map format and a minimal editor.** Represent the map as a 2D array of cell codes (terrain, road, water, bridge, building type, turret, base, flag) in JSON or a custom Resource; a `MapLoader` fills a `GridMap` from a `MeshLibrary` and spawns entity scenes; author maps either by painting the `GridMap` in the editor and exporting cell codes, or with a tiny in-game editor that writes JSON. Docs: [Using GridMaps](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/3d/using_gridmaps.html), [GridMap](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_gridmap.html), [JSON](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_json.html), [Saving games](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/io/saving_games.html) (FileAccess + JSON pattern), [TileMapLayer](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_tilemaplayer.html) if you want a 2D editor view.

**6. Destructibles, audio, menus, polish.** Buildings/bridges with hit points swap to wrecked scenes and clear/alter GridMap cells; debris `RigidBody3D`s with `apply_impulse()`; `AudioStreamPlayer3D` for positional engine/weapon sounds, `AudioStreamPlayer` for music; menus with Control nodes, containers, themes, and gamepad focus navigation. Docs: [Creating instances](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/getting_started/step_by_step/instancing.html), [RigidBody3D](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_rigidbody3d.html), [Audio streams](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/audio/audio_streams.html), [Using Containers](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/ui/gui_containers.html), [Keyboard/Controller navigation and focus](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/ui/gui_navigation.html), [Introduction to GUI skinning](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/ui/gui_skinning.html), [AnimationTree](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/animation/animation_tree.html) for turret/rotor animation states.

**7. Packaging for Windows, macOS, Linux (and Steam).** Install export templates (Editor > Manage Export Templates). Windows exports bundle a `data.pck` with an optimised binary and can be produced from any host OS ([Exporting for Windows](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/export/exporting_for_windows.html)); Linux exports likewise, for x86_64/arm64 and more ([Exporting for Linux](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/export/exporting_for_linux.html)); macOS exports are Universal 2 (Intel + Apple silicon), need a unique bundle identifier, and "Projects exported without code signing and notarization will be blocked by Gatekeeper if they are downloaded from unknown sources" — notarization requires an Apple Developer ID certificate ([Exporting for macOS](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/export/exporting_for_macos.html)). Steam: the Steam Direct fee is "$100.00 USD fee for each product", recoupable once the product has "at least $1,000.00 USD Adjusted Gross Revenue", with a 30-day wait after paying, a 1–5 day review and a "coming soon" page for at least two weeks ([Steam Direct](https://partner.steamgames.com/steamdirect)). Remote Play Together "allows users to invite their Steam Friends to join the game as though they were sitting at the same computer", only the host needs the game, and it is automatically enabled for games tagged Local Multiplayer, Local Co-op or Shared/Split Screen ([Remote Play Together](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/features/remoteplay)) — so a local split-screen game gets online couch play essentially for free. Steamworks API access from Godot is via the third-party GodotSteam module/GDExtension ([godotsteam.com](https://godotsteam.com/), the project's own site; **Unverified:** its licence).

- **Blender.** Current download is Blender 5.2.2 LTS (15 Sep 2026) with an Apple silicon build ([blender.org/download](https://www.blender.org/download/)); **Unverified:** whether an Intel macOS build is still offered (not listed on the page as fetched). Godot's export guidance: add a Triangulate modifier and make sure "Apply Modifiers is checked in the export dialog"; in Blender "+Y is rear and -Y is front for an asset" ([Model export considerations](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/assets_pipeline/importing_3d_scenes/model_export_considerations.html)). Low-poly wasteland style: flat-shaded meshes with a single small palette texture, rust/sand tones, decals for damage — cheap to make and to render in split screen.

You're free to use them, even in commercial projects", attribution "not required" ([Kenney support FAQ](https://kenney.nl/support)); e.g. the Car Kit is labelled "Creative Commons CC0" ([Car Kit](https://kenney.nl/assets/car-kit)), and the catalogue includes City Kit (Industrial/Roads) and Modular Cave Kit ([assets](https://kenney.nl/assets)). (CC0)" ([Ultimate Platformer Pack](https://quaternius.itch.io/ultimate-platformer-pack))), and the catalogue includes Cars, Animated Tanks and a Zombie Apocalypse Kit ([quaternius.com](https://quaternius.com/)). Keep a `CREDITS.md` anyway — CC0 does not require it, but it is good practice and covers the Godot MIT notice.
- **Importing glTF.** Godot: "glTF 2.0 (recommended)", both `.gltf` and `.glb`; `.blend` files import directly by calling Blender; FBX via ufbx; OBJ and COLLADA supported ([Available 3D formats](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/assets_pipeline/importing_3d_scenes/available_formats.html)). Unreal: assets import through the Interchange framework via the Content Browser or File > Import Into Level (glTF and MaterialX) ([Importing assets using Interchange](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/importing-assets-using-interchange-in-unreal-engine)); glTF 2.0 support covers `.gltf` and `.glb` and several KHR extensions ([glTF file format support](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/gltf-file-format-support-in-unreal-engine)). Bevy: `bevy::gltf` provides "an AssetLoader and type definitions for loading glTF 2.0", spawned via `GltfAssetLabel::Scene(0)` ([bevy::gltf](https://docs.rs/bevy/latest/bevy/gltf/index.html)).

### Remake vs. spiritual successor — the legal line
1. What copyright does not protect. "In no case does copyright protection for an original work of authorship extend to any idea, procedure, process, system, method of operation, concept, principle, or discovery, regardless of the form in which it is described, explained, illustrated, or embodied in such work." ([17 U.S.C. § 102(b)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/102)). The Copyright Office: "Copyright does not protect ideas, concepts, systems, or methods of doing something." ([Copyright Office FAQ](https://www.copyright.gov/help/faq/faq-protect.html)); "copyright protection will extend only to the original expression in that work and not to the underlying idea, methods, or systems described or explained" ([Circular 33, "Works Not Protected by Copyright", rev. 03/2021](https://www.copyright.gov/circs/circ33.pdf)). The Office's (now retired) games factsheet was explicit: "The idea for a game is not protected by copyright. The same is true of the name or title given to the game and of the method or methods for playing it ... Once a game has been made public, nothing in the copyright law prevents others from developing another game based on similar principles." ([FL-108 "Games", June 1999, archived](https://web.archive.org/web/20030210123252/http://www.copyright.gov/fls/fl108.html)).
2. What it does protect. The same sources protect "the particular manner of an author's expression in literary, artistic, or musical form" (FL-108) — i.e. Return Fire's code, its sprites/polygon models, box and logo art, level layouts as drawn, sound effects, the skull, the video clips, and the EMI recordings. Copying any of those without a licence is infringement regardless of what you call the game.
3. Where "look and feel" bites. In Tetris Holding v. Xio Interactive (D.N.J. 2012) the court restated that "The game mechanics and the rules are not entitled to protection, but courts have found expressive elements copyrightable", then found infringement because the clone copied the expression wholesale — piece shapes and colours, "the dimensions of the playing field", ghost pieces, next-piece display, etc.: "It is the wholesale copying of the Tetris look that the Court finds troubling more than the individual similarities each considered in isolation." Summary judgment was also granted on the trade-dress claim ([opinion, GovInfo PDF](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-njd-3_09-cv-06115/pdf/USCOURTS-njd-3_09-cv-06115-0.pdf); [CourtListener copy](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/8715588/tetris-holding-llc-v-xio-interactive-inc/)). Lesson: four asymmetric vehicles, one fragile flag carrier, hidden flag towers, fuel/ammo depots, split-screen CTF — all usable. Recreating Return Fire's specific art, HUD, maps, skull, sounds and music arrangement together is not.
4. Names and trade dress are trademark questions, not copyright. Titles are uncopyrightable ("The title or subtitle of a work") but "may be protectable under federal or state trademark laws" ([Circular 33](https://www.copyright.gov/circs/circ33.pdf)). Unregistered trade dress is actionable under the Lanham Act where use of a "word, term, name, symbol, or device ... is likely to cause confusion ... as to the origin, sponsorship, or approval of his or her goods" ([15 U.S.C. § 1125(a)(1)(A)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1125)); for unregistered trade dress "the person who asserts trade dress protection has the burden of proving that the matter sought to be protected is not functional" ([§ 1125(a)(3)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1125)), and product-design trade dress "is distinctive, and therefore protectible, only upon a showing of secondary meaning" ([Wal-Mart Stores v. Samara Bros., 529 U.S. 205 (2000)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/99-150.ZO.html)).

Plain-language summary for the user:
- "Remake" using the name RETURN FIRE, the logo, the original art, code, sounds, videos or the EMI recordings needs three separate permissions:
  - the copyright owner (Silent Software, Inc. or whoever holds it now) for code, art, sounds, maps and video;
  - Reef Entertainment Limited for the name RETURN FIRE in the US and UK (live registrations, Classes 9 and 41);
  - the owner of the EMI Classics/Angel masters for the recordings.
  Without all three, do not ship it.
- "Spiritual successor" with an original name and original assets that reuses the ruleset (vehicle-based CTF, four vehicle classes, hidden flag towers, fuel/ammo, destructible terrain, split screen):
  - rules and mechanics are outside copyright (§ 102(b), Circular 33, FL-108, Tetris v. Xio);
  - the remaining risks are copying protectable expression too closely (art, HUD, exact maps, sounds, music arrangement as a whole) and marketing that implies affiliation with Return Fire or Reef;
  - refer to the inspiration in press as a factual statement if at all, never in the title, logo or store name.

## F. Audio licensing for the classical-music flavour

### Composition vs. recording: two separate copyrights
- The Copyright Office: "the song 'Rolling in the Deep' authored by Adele and Paul Epworth and a recording of Aretha Franklin singing 'Rolling in the Deep' are two distinct works. The underlying music and lyrics are a 'musical work,' and a recording of an artist performing that song is a 'sound recording.'" ([Circular 56, "Copyright Registration for Sound Recordings", rev. 03/2021](https://www.copyright.gov/circs/circ56.pdf)).
- Compositions: for pre-1978 works the maximum US term is 95 years from publication, so "all works published in the United States before January 1, 1931, are in the public domain" ([Circular 15A, "Duration of Copyright", rev. 04/2026](https://www.copyright.gov/circs/circ15a.pdf)). Holst, Grieg, Wagner, Rimsky-Korsakov, Handel, Rossini and Verdi scores are therefore free to use, arrange, and re-record.
- Recordings: a 1970s–80s EMI master is not. Even pre-1972 recordings now have federal protection: "The federal remedies for unauthorized use of Pre-1972 Sound Recordings shall be available for 95 years after the year of first publication of the recording, subject to certain additional periods" — recordings published 1923–1946 get 5 extra years, 1947–1956 get 15 extra years, and those published 1957 through 14 Feb 1972 are protected until 15 Feb 2067 ([US Copyright Office, Pre-1972 Sound Recordings](https://www.copyright.gov/music-modernization/pre1972-soundrecordings/)). Applying that schedule (my arithmetic, not a quote): pre-1923 recordings became US public domain on 1 Jan 2022, and 1923–1946 recordings follow one year at a time after 100 years, so as of 2026 only recordings first published in 1925 or earlier are free in the US. Do not assume an "old" orchestral CD is free.

### Sources of freely usable classical recordings
- Musopen. Musopen describes itself as "a 501(c)(3) non-profit ... We provide recordings, sheet music, and textbooks to the public for free, without copyright restrictions" ([Musopen's own text on its archive.org collection page](https://archive.org/details/musopen)). Its 2012 Kickstarter recordings (Beethoven, Brahms, Grieg's Peer Gynt, Rimsky-Korsakov, Tchaikovsky, Smetana, Mozart, string quartets and more) were "recorded and released into the public" domain, with the archive.org copies tagged with the Creative Commons Public Domain Mark ([The Musopen DVD](https://archive.org/details/musopen-dvd); [Musopen Lossless DVD](https://archive.org/details/musopen-lossless-dvd)); recordings uploaded from a musopen.org account carry the same Public Domain Mark ([example: Chopin Waltz Op. 69 No. 1, uploader musopen.org](https://archive.org/details/MusopenChopinWaltzOp69no1)). **Unverified:** the current wording of Musopen's own licensing page — [musopen.org/music/licensing/](https://musopen.org/music/licensing/) — which sits behind a bot-check and could not be retrieved; read it directly, and check the licence shown on each individual track, because Musopen also hosts material under other licences (e.g. the "Musopen and Libre Classic Music" set on archive.org is CC BY-SA 3.0: [item](https://archive.org/details/Musopen-Libre)).
- Note on the Public Domain Mark: it is an identification, not a licence — "This work has been identified as being free of known restrictions under copyright law" and "The work may not be free of known copyright restrictions in all jurisdictions" ([CC Public Domain Mark 1.0](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/mark/1.0/)). Prefer tracks with an explicit CC0 dedication when you can.
- Open Goldberg Variations (Bach BWV 988, Kimiko Ishizaka, released 28 May 2012): "All tracks are provided without copyright (Creative Commons Zero). All uses permitted." ([album page](https://kimikoishizaka.bandcamp.com/album/j-s-bach-open-goldberg-variations-bwv-988-piano); project site: [opengoldbergvariations.org](https://www.opengoldbergvariations.org/)). Piano Bach is a different flavour from Return Fire's brass-heavy orchestral cues, but it is a clean CC0 model of what to look for.
- CC0 means: "The person who associated a work with this deed has dedicated the work to the public domain by waiving all of his or her rights to the work worldwide under copyright law ... You can copy, modify, distribute and perform the work, even for commercial purposes, all without asking permission." It does not touch "patent or trademark rights of any person" or "publicity or privacy rights" ([CC0 1.0 deed](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/)).
- The alternative used by the original: commission or record your own performances (or MIDI/orchestral-library renditions) of the public-domain scores. You then own the recording. The designer's original plan before the EMI deal was exactly this — "an approximate budget of $15,000 to fly out to Europe and have a selection of classical themes scored for the game" ([Retro Gaming Blog interview](https://theretrogamingblog.wordpress.com/2015/05/14/reichart-von-wolfsheild-3do-return-fire-and-the-industry/)).

### Sound effects: Freesound, and CC0 vs CC BY
- Freesound distributes sounds under Creative Commons licences and explains them: for CC0 "you can do pretty much what you want with the sound. You could even sell the sound, ... but you can't claim you are the author!"; "For 'attribution' you should always mention the original creators of the sounds when you use them"; "'Noncommercial' works like attribution, but you can't earn any money with the piece of work you create!"; suggested credit form: "This [video/theatre piece/...] uses these sounds from freesound..." with links to each sound and its licence ([Freesound FAQ, licences section](https://freesound.org/help/faq/)).
- CC BY 4.0 obligations, from Creative Commons: you may "copy and redistribute the material in any medium or format for any purpose, even commercially" and "remix, transform, and build upon the material", provided "You must give appropriate credit, provide a link to the license, and indicate if changes were made" and "You may not apply legal terms or technological measures that legally restrict others from doing anything the license permits" ([CC BY 4.0 deed](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/)). CC0 has no attribution condition ([CC0 1.0 deed](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/)).
- Practical rule for the project:
  - filter Freesound to CC0 for anything you do not want to credit;
  - if you take CC BY sounds, keep a credits file (author, sound URL, licence URL, whether modified) and ship it in the game;
  - never use CC BY-NC material in a game you might sell;
  - keep the same manifest for music tracks, recording the performer, source page and licence for each.
