-- CONTRADICTIONS
* 'The Motorbike's cream headlamp at its nose is the heading cue and stays visible': CONTRADICTED. The kit headlamp is one 0.22 m lens facing -Z, 0 pixels from the chase camera at 13 m and at 40 m (32 px at the front at 13 m), against 260/16/1,872 px for the greybox nose. A cream front fender is also invisible from behind; a cream tank-top patch gives 48 px at 13 m.
* 'Each is merged into a few meshes (one per material)': one surface per kit material is 11 to 16 surfaces per Unit and 117 draw calls per view for two Units (4-split shadows), 3.2x the 4-surface merge (37). Accents can be one surface only with per-vertex tone; neutrals fold into one vertex-coloured surface.
* 'The Motorbike carries the Flag without hiding its cream nose' (AC-12, story evidence list): the kit bike has no cream nose, and a Flag at carry_offset (0,0.5,1.7) covers the bike's rear and middle from the chase camera, floats at y=0.5 and overlaps the tail spike tips by 0.11 m for a 0.45 m radius.
* Merging is 'visually identical' only for geometry: with the kit textures 62 to 77% of model pixels differ because the object-space triplanar paint-chip pattern is re-anchored to the model origin.
* 'Keeping the kit's textured paint with the Team colour as albedo' (tinted copies) is not clean at 40 m: the kit textures have no mipmaps and speckle; it also needs a Unit hook (not material_override) and 12 to 14 surfaces.
* 'A Flag, a Fuel Can and a Unit of either Team colour told apart at 40 m over sand, salt flat, canyon road and ford': Orange over the canyon road (distance 0.29, luminance ratio 1.05) and sand (0.35/1.28) and Teal over the ford (0.48/1.43) are weak pairs; the thin Orange rotor nearly vanishes over road and sand. Flag not tested.
* Building at every Unit.spawn() (the Unit swaps its Model every spawn and GarageQueue calls spawn() at every respawn) costs 33 to 63 ms windowed, 2 to 4 frames; a headless run shows only 3.5 to 6 ms.
-- GUARDRAILS
* Never put a raw kit scene into play: two raw Units cost 1,165 draw calls per view (2,330 both views, 294 visible + 871 shadow per view), 2.3x the whole 1,000 budget.

-- SUMMARY
The four kit models can go into play as Unit models, with three conditions: merge them, build the merge once and cache it, and drop the Motorbike's cream-nose heading cue. Raw kit Units cost 1,165 draw calls per view for two Units (2,330 for both views, against the 1,000 budget). Merged one surface per kit material they cost 117 per view. Folding every Team accent into one vertex-toned Accent mesh and every neutral into one vertex-coloured surface gives 4 surfaces per Unit and 37 draw calls per view, against 29 for the greybox models. The merge is geometry-identical to the kit: 13 to 196 differing pixels out of 38k to 130k model pixels when untextured. With the kit textures the pixels differ, because the object-space triplanar paint-chip pattern lands in a different place. The real Unit.spawn() and Unit._paint_team_colour() paint a merged Accent mesh in group team_colour unchanged, and Neutral stays unpainted. Rebuilding at every spawn costs 33 to 63 ms (26 to 53 ms of it is GPU readback in get_mesh_arrays()); a cached respawn costs 0.1 ms. Contradicted: the kit Motorbike's cream headlamp is invisible from the chase camera (0 pixels at 13 m and 40 m, against 260 and 16 for the greybox nose). The Flag at carry_offset (0, 0.5, 1.7) overlaps the tail spikes by 0.11 m for a 0.45 m Flag radius. Tinted textured paint (the kit's own materials copied with the Team colour as albedo) speckles at 40 m because the kit textures have no mipmaps. Orange over the canyon road and sand is the weakest ground contrast. Frame rate, the Flag at 40 m and the older baselines are UNKNOWN. A tested reference implementation and all evidence images are in the scratch directory (see recommendation). The repo and assets/ are untouched (git status shows only the two files that were already modified).

-- CONTRADICTIONS
* The brief's premise that a NoiseTexture2D is generated in a thread and may leave the first frames blank: measured get_image() null at frame 0 but ready after 1 frame (12.7-92.7 ms each, about 300 ms for nine), frame 1 took 283-321 ms against 16 ms with ImageTextures, and 0 of 576 samples were magenta/blank in 11 windowed runs. The first generation is effectively synchronous.
* The story's positions table says the sea panel to X -162..162, Z -60..60 means 'no camera sees the world's edge': from the Gyrocopter chase pose at the west tip the panel ends as a hard sea/sky line 23 m ahead (r1_chase_sea); a +-700 m quad removes it at the same one draw call.
* The intent that a Flag, a Fuel Can and a Unit of either Team colour are told apart at 40 m (including the Gyrocopter over water) with a palette that follows the drawing: Teal over a sea like the drawing's (hue 192-196, luminance 0.42) measured dE76 22-29 and luminance ratio 1.0-1.1; the drawing's Fuel Can colour (0.70,0.26,0.11) is only dE 23 from Team Orange. It holds only with a navy sea (dE 44-46) and a cooler can red (dE 33).
* The intent to copy the Sun and WorldEnvironment from greybox_field.tscn while keeping one palette that follows the drawing: that light renders horizontal ground at about 1.5x its linear albedo, so the drawing's colours used as albedo come out too bright and the salt flat (0.88,0.85,0.77 -> 0.99,0.97,0.91) and ford (-> 0.73,1.00,1.00) clip.
* Seamless declarative tiling for the cracked salt flat: the seamless NoiseTexture2D cellular recipe leaves ghost wedges at the tile edge (seam ratio 0.36-0.48) and repeats obviously every 32 m (about 6 times over the flat); the periodic script Voronoi tiles exactly but looks like paving.
-- GUARDRAILS
* Build every ground, rock, water and wall texture as a hand-written NoiseTexture2D .tres: it matches the script route to within 1 level (max diff 1), is byte-identical across processes (44/44 PNGs) and between headless and windowed (10/10), and is textured on the first drawn frame.
* Never read NoiseTexture2D.get_image() in _ready(): it was null at frame 0 for all 11 recipes and ready after one frame; await get_tree().process_frame (or the texture's changed signal) first.
* Expect about 0.3 s of main-thread work in the first frame for the nine textures (12.7-92.7 ms each, first generation synchronous) and do not instantiate NoiseTexture2D resources mid-round.
* Never use a seamless cellular NoiseTexture2D for the cracked salt flat (seam ratio 0.36-0.48, ghost wedges, 32 m repeat); use one non-tiling 2048x388 salt_span texture mapped with UV 0..1 over the 190x36 m quad (9.3 cm per texel, 4.2 MB, 32 ms).
* Set texture_filter = TEXTURE_FILTER_LINEAR_WITH_MIPMAPS_ANISOTROPIC on ground materials (the default is LINEAR_WITH_MIPMAPS = 3; the viewports already default to 4x anisotropy); it adds 14-17% frame-to-frame change (0.0060 to 0.0070) and nothing shimmers either way.
* Call Image.generate_mipmaps() before ImageTexture.create_from_image() on any script-made texture, because the author's mottle() returns a texture with no mipmaps.
* Give ground meshes world-metre UVs (UV = (x, z)) with uv1_scale = 1/tile_m, and use object-space triplanar on BoxMesh blocks; never node-scale a MeshInstance3D that uses object-space triplanar or plain UVs on a BoxMesh (both measured stretched), use BoxMesh.size or uv1_world_triplanar = true.
* Stack ground overlays at least 5 mm apart (use 10-30 mm): coplanar overlays z-fought (0.80 of samples correct at 70-130 m), 0.5 mm and above were clean.
* With the greybox Sun (1.0) and ambient (0.7) author ground ramps divided by 1.48 in linear space (render equals the drawing: sand (0.80,0.64,0.43), salt (0.86,0.83,0.76), road (0.72,0.53,0.35)); lowering the light to Sun 0.7 / ambient 0.45 also works but darkens the Orange side face from (0.85,0.45,0.18) to (0.72,0.37,0.15).
* Make the water navy (render about (0.07,0.20,0.35), hue about 212 degrees), not the drawing's teal-cyan: Teal over it measured dE 44-46 and luminance ratio 1.9-2.2 against dE 22-29 and about 1.0 over the bright sea.
* Make the Fuel Can red cooler than the drawing's red-orange (0.70,0.26,0.11): authored dE76 from Team Orange is 23.2, against 30.8 for (0.80,0.13,0.10) and 33.4 for (0.78,0.08,0.10).
* Lay the sea as a quad or plane of at least +-700 m: the 324x120 m panel shows a hard sea/sky edge 23 m ahead from the west-tip chase pose.
* Budget about 2.2 draw calls per distinct-mesh MeshInstance3D (both views, shadows on at 100 m, cameras along the axis): 1,000 draw calls is about 446 instances in the world and 400 per view about 355 (the 58-mesh map with no props already costs 147).
* Share one BoxMesh and one material resource between identical props (300 boxes: 795 -> 315 draw calls), set cast_shadow OFF on ground meshes (-38), keep directional_shadow_max_distance at 100 (160 adds 208, 250 adds 405 draw calls) and consider PARALLEL_2_SPLITS (-78).
* Do not spend effort on material count (6, 10 and 24 materials all 795 draw calls with distinct meshes) or on ground triplanar versus UV (147 draw calls and the same frame time both ways).
* Use MultiMeshInstance3D only for dense small static props: draw calls stay flat at 187 but every instance is drawn in every view (primitives 39,112 and 75,112 against 10,888 and 19,144).
* Judge cost by draw calls and total frame time: RenderingServer.viewport_get_measured_render_time_gpu returned 0.00 for both viewports here.

-- SUMMARY
The whole ground look can be built from engine resources only: nine hand-written NoiseTexture2D .tres files (FastNoiseLite + Gradient) plus SurfaceTool/BoxMesh geometry reproduce the illustrated drawing from above, load with correct parameters, are byte-identical across processes and between headless and windowed runs (44/44 and 10/10), and match the GDScript route to within 1 level, so the declarative route is recommended. The brief's thread/blank-frame worry is contradicted: the first generation is effectively synchronous (ready after 1 frame, 13-93 ms each, about 0.3 s on the first frame, never a magenta frame). Four findings change the build: seamless cellular cracks ghost and visibly repeat (use one non-tiling 2048x388 salt_span texture); the greybox light renders ground about 1.5x too bright (author ramps divided by 1.48 in linear space, the final set renders sand (0.80,0.64,0.43), salt (0.86,0.83,0.76), road (0.72,0.53,0.35)); the drawing's teal-cyan sea and red-orange Fuel Can fail the 40 m check (Teal over it dE 22-29, can vs Orange dE 23) while a navy sea gives dE 44-46; and the 324x120 m sea panel shows a hard edge 23 m ahead, so the sea needs a +-700 m plane. Cost: draw calls, not frame time, are the limit (1.8-2.3 ms per frame even at 1,483 draw calls): about 2.2 per distinct-mesh MeshInstance3D (about 355 instances under 400 per view), sharing meshes (795 -> 315), shadow distance and shadows on/off dominate, material count does not matter, and GPU time could not be read on this driver.
