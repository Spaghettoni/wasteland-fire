Story 012 AC-9: how the damaged look was chosen (2026-10-08)
Two technical-artist agents built the developer's default (two stages at 80% and 35%, darker and more cracked, chunks at the foot) in separate scratch copies, rendered with the look probe (measurements/flag_walls_look_probe.gd.txt, look-probe-render.sh.txt); an art-director judge compared the frames at play distance. The orchestrator then applied the judge's five tweaks to the winner (B), re-rendered and read the frames (look-probe/), and integrated it.

== attempt A
damaged_1: albedo_color = Color(0.38, 0.38, 0.38, 1), below wall.tres's 0.55. It keeps the wall's own albedo_texture scrap.tres and wall.tres's mapping: uv1_scale 0.0625, uv1_triplanar and uv1_world_triplanar true, roughness 1.0, texture_filter 5. Cracks come from the BaseMaterial3D detail layer: detail_enabled true, detail_blend_mode 0 (MIX). detail_mask is flag_wall_cracks_1.tres, a 512 px NoiseTexture2D of FastNoiseLite cellular noise (noise_type 2, Manhattan distance, jitter 0.9, cellular_return_type 4, seed 113, frequency 0.022). Its colour ramp is white at 0 to 0.09 and fades to black at 0.2, which gives thin crack lines. detail_albedo is flag_wall_soot.tres, a 256 px simplex noise with a near-black brown ramp, so the cracks become black fissures. The detail mask and albedo follow the same world-triplanar uv1, so the cracks wrap the panel in world space. Chunks: 4 chips (Chunk1-4), 2 on each face, in wall.tres.
damaged_2: albedo_color = Color(0.3, 0.3, 0.3, 1), below damaged 1's 0.38, with the same triplanar mapping, roughness and filter. The albedo_texture is flag_wall_scorched.tres. It uses the exact noise of scrap.tres (simplex, seed 71, frequency 0.045, 4-octave fbm), so the wall's own pattern stays, but the lowest 45% of its ramp is pulled to char black-brown. Cracks also use the detail layer (MIX). detail_mask is flag_wall_cracks_2.tres: cellular Manhattan noise, seed 131, frequency 0.034, which makes denser cells than damaged 1. Its ramp is fully white up to 0.2 (wider cracks), then a soot halo at 45% that fades out by 0.48, which gives the scorching. detail_albedo is flag_wall_fissures_2.tres, built from the identical cellular noise so it lines up exactly with the mask. Its ramp is a black core up to 0.09, a wall-brown rim (0.24, 0.14, 0.09) from 0.12 to 0.17, and soot beyond 0.21. The result is black fissures with brown broken edges, which stay visible on the charred base; with plain black fissures (round 1) the panel read as a solid black block. Chunks: 8 chips (Chunk1-8). The first four sit at damaged 1's places and four more are added, 4 on each face, in wall.tres.
chunks: One new sub_resource, BoxMesh_chip, size Vector3(0.55, 0.25, 0.42), smaller than the rubble's 0.9 x 0.3 x 0.7 in every dimension and sized in the mesh with no node scale. Its material is wall.tres, so the chips read as broken-off wall like the Rubble. All 12 chunk nodes share it, each with cast_shadow = 0 and no collider anywhere under either look (checked: 4 and 8 MeshInstance3D children, 0 collision nodes).
- Counts: MeshDamaged1 has 4 (Chunk1-4), MeshDamaged2 has 8 (Chunk1-8).
- Height: every chunk sits at local y = -0.875 (= -1 + half height 0.125), with yaw from -38 to 40 degrees and tilt or roll of 6 degrees or less. Computed tops are 0.264 to 0.278 m above the ground.
- Placement: centres are at |z| 0.80 to 0.98 and |x| 1.25 or less, half at z > 0 and half at z < 0.
- Damaged 1 places (x, z): (-0.6, 0.85), (0.95, 0.82), (-0.3, -0.86), (1.1, -0.82).
- Damaged 2 adds: (0.2, 0.95), (-1.25, 0.8), (-1.15, -0.9), (0.45, -0.98).
From above, each chip is about 8 x 6 px of brown on sand.
readability_above: The near panel's top face, measured as mean sRGB at 1x: whole (65, 40, 30), first look (43, 31, 27), second look (37, 31, 28).
- Whole vs either damaged look: clear. The top face is visibly darker and the brown chips stand out on the sand.
- First look vs whole: it reads through the darker top and its 2 chips per face. Its cracks do not show on the roughly 16 px wide top face at play distance, so at 1x the chips do most of the work.
- First vs second look: this is the weakest pair. The average darkness gap is small (43 vs 37), so they are told apart by two things. The second look's top shows the brown-rimmed fissure grid on charred black (a visible texture at 1x, clear at 2x). And its chips form a row of 4 per face instead of 2.
- Second look vs fallen: unmistakable. The panel still stands with its shadow, while the fallen state shows the big rubble chunks.
Side walls in the all-looks frames read the same way: chips on both faces, and the second look's grid visible.
readability_chase: The near front face, measured as mean sRGB in the left half: whole (50, 30, 21), first look (32, 23, 19), second look (27, 22, 20).
- Whole: a medium mottled brown.
- First look: clearly darker than whole. A thin black crack network is visible but subtle at 1x, and 2 brown chips sit at the foot, one partly hidden by the car. It reads as a darker, lightly cracked wall rather than a strongly cracked one.
- Second look: the strongest read. A charred, near-black panel with an obvious network of brown-edged fissures and a row of 4 chips at the foot. It is clearly different from both whole and the first look, still a solid standing block, and never reads as passable.
- Fallen: unchanged rubble.
concerns: docs/engine-reference/godot/ does not exist in the scratch copy, so I could not check properties against it. Instead I verified every StandardMaterial3D property by ClassDB introspection on the installed 4.7.2: all exist. Their separation there depends on the second look's fissure grid and the chip count. The strongest fix I would try next is the first look back at about 0.42 with crisp thin cracks: round 1 measured 47 from above and had the most visible chase cracks. That combination was not rendered because of the 4-round cap. | The first look's cracks do not read from above at play distance and are subtle in chase. Its readability from above rests on the darkness step and the 2 chips. | The second look is near-black, and its Manhattan cellular fissure grid is fairly regular, close to the Map's cracked-floor style. The brown rims and wall.tres chips tie it to the wall family, but whether it reads as 'the same wall, hurt' is an art-director call. | The delivered frames are round 2's render. After round 4 I regenerated the files from cfg-2.json; the generator is deterministic, so the text is identical, but I did not re-render because of the 4-round cap. The probe reported checks=0, so it asserted nothing. - Textures: five runtime NoiseTexture2D textures, four at 512 px RGBA and one at 256 px. That is about 5.6 MiB of VRAM with mipmaps, generated on load in a thread.
- Shaders: the detail layer adds 2 material shader variants.
- Draw calls: a damaged wall draws its panel plus 4 or 8 small chip meshes; hidden looks are not drawn. I have not checked whether Forward+ batches the identical chip meshes. | Minor: Chunk4 (x 1.1, z -0.82, yaw 28 degrees), present in both looks, reaches |z| 0.497 at its rotated corner, so it touches the panel face by about 3 mm. Its centre meets the |z| > 0.5 rule.

== attempt B
damaged_1: albedo_color = Color(0.38, 0.38, 0.38, 1), below wall.tres's 0.55, applied to the same scrap.tres albedo_texture. The material keeps roughness 1.0, uv1_scale 0.0625, uv1_triplanar and uv1_world_triplanar on, and texture_filter 5.

Cracks: detail_enabled = true, detail_blend_mode = 0 (MIX), detail_mask = textures/flag_wall_cracks_1.tres, detail_albedo = textures/flag_wall_scar.tres. Both are sampled through the uv1 world triplanar mapping (16 m per tile, 32 texels per metre).
- The mask is a cellular FastNoiseLite: noise_type 2, return type 4 (F2-F1), euclidean distance, jitter 0.9, frequency 0.02, which gives crack cells of about 1.6 m.
- A domain warp makes the lines jagged: simplex, amplitude 9, frequency 0.04, progressive, 3 octaves.
- A white-to-black ramp at offsets 0.05 to 0.09 turns the noise into scar lines about 0.1 m wide.
- The scar albedo is a simplex ramp from rust (0.55, 0.32, 0.18) through (0.64, 0.40, 0.24) to pale scrap (0.62, 0.50, 0.40).

Chunks: 4 chips, 2 on each face, under MeshDamaged1 (Chunk1 to Chunk4), lying flat at y -0.87.
- Chunk1 at x -0.9, z 0.85, turned 20 degrees about Y.
- Chunk2 at x 0.75, z 0.88, turned -35 degrees.
- Chunk3 at x 0.55, z -0.86, turned -25 degrees.
- Chunk4 at x -0.7, z -0.88, turned 35 degrees.
damaged_2: albedo_color = Color(0.26, 0.26, 0.26, 1), below damaged 1's 0.38, on the same scrap.tres base with the same uv1 world triplanar mapping, roughness and filter.

Cracks: the same MIX detail setup, with detail_mask = textures/flag_wall_cracks_2.tres and the same flag_wall_scar.tres detail_albedo.
- The mask is cellular F2-F1 at frequency 0.026, about 1.2 m cells, so more cracks than damaged 1.
- The ramp runs from 0.085 to 0.135, so the scars are wider, about 0.13 m.
- The domain warp is stronger, amplitude 11.
- About 20% of the face is light scar, against about 12% for damaged 1.

Chunks: 8 chips, 4 on each face. Chunk1 to Chunk4 repeat damaged 1's four places, and Chunk5 to Chunk8 grow them into piles.
- Chunk5 at x -0.35, y -0.82, z 1.0, turned 50 degrees about Y and tilted 10 degrees.
- Chunk6 at x 1.15, y -0.82, z 0.95, turned -60 degrees about Y and tilted -10 degrees.
- Chunk7 at x 1.1, y -0.87, z -1.0, turned -10 degrees about Y.
- Chunk8 at x -0.15, y -0.82, z -1.02, turned 70 degrees about Y and tilted 10 degrees.
chunks: The chip mesh is one new sub_resource, BoxMesh_chip, sized 0.6 x 0.26 x 0.44 m in the mesh (smaller than the rubble's 0.9 x 0.3 x 0.7), shared by all 12 chunk nodes. Its material is a new materials/flag_wall_chips.tres: the scar texture at albedo_color 0.9, world triplanar, roughness 1, so the chips show the light broken tone. Every chunk has cast_shadow = 0 and there are no colliders under either look.

Counts: MeshDamaged1 has 4 chunks (2 on each face), MeshDamaged2 has 8 (4 on each face).

Measured in-engine on the chunks' boxes, with each look at y = 1:
- Flat chips sit at local y -0.87 and their tops are 0.26 m above the ground.
- Chips tilted 10 degrees sit at -0.82 and their tops are 0.36 m above the ground, under the 0.4 m limit.
- Every bottom is at 0.0, on the ground.
- The smallest |local z| of a centre is 0.85, outside the 1 m thick panel. The largest |local x| is 1.15, inside 1.4.

The rotations are written as exact rotation matrices to 7 decimals, so every chunk's node scale is 1.
readability_above: Damaged 2 reads well in the near view from above. The near face is visibly darker and covered by a dense network of light rust cracks, and a pile of 4 light chips sits on the outer foot, plus 4 more on the inner side when they are in view. It is clearly told apart from both the whole wall and damaged 1.

Damaged 1 is the weakest case. In above_near_first_look it is told apart from the whole wall by a light scar network on the visible face and the top, and by 2 chips per face (each about 9 x 7 px). At a glance it is moderate, not strong. Its darker panel (0.38 against 0.55) hardly registers from above, because the light scars bring the average brightness back up. The scars and the chips carry the read, not darkness.

Told apart from each other, damaged 1 and damaged 2 differ by crack density, panel darkness and 4 against 8 chips. That is clear side by side and less clear for a single wall seen alone.

In the two all-looks frames, the Gate-side wall's look reads. The far Garage wall and the side walls sit partly under the water tower's legs and crossbars and show mostly their 1 m top faces. There, damaged 1 against damaged 2 comes down to chip count and density, which is hard to see at 50-60 px. Whole against fallen stays obvious.

The chips read as small rust-red bits, darker and redder than the sand.
readability_chase: All three looks read clearly in the chase view. The whole wall is plain dark scrap. Damaged 1 is a darker face with a network of jagged light rust scars, and chips show at its foot as light bits, partly behind the truck's turret in this framing. Damaged 2 is a near-black brown face crazed all over by a denser, wider light network, with a visibly bigger pile of chips. The fallen look is unchanged rubble.

Each damaged look is told apart from the whole wall and from the other at once, and both still read as the same solid wall: the silhouette and face shading are intact, and the scars are darker and redder than the sand, so nothing looks like a gap.

Up close the pattern, damaged 2 especially, reads partly as a giraffe or crazed-lava print rather than cracks. The domain warp reduced this but did not remove it. In round 4, damaged 1 is a sparser version of damaged 2's network. Round 3 (kept in backup-r3) had a more natural "a few cracks" damaged 1 here, at the cost of being less readable from above.
concerns: The engine reference (docs/engine-reference/godot/) is not in the scratch copy, and the real repo's reference has nothing on BaseMaterial3D detail properties, NoiseTexture2D or FastNoiseLite. I verified the properties against the 4.7.2 binary's property list instead (look-B/tools/propcheck2.txt). That check found one wrong name: the property is detail_uv_layer, not detail_uv_layout. Godot dropped my detail_uv_layout line with a warning ('Godot 3.x SpatialMaterial remapped parameter not found'). It was only the default, so I removed it. Every property in the delivered files was confirmed on the render. | I added a third material, materials/flag_wall_chips.tres, beyond the two the task names. The chips are separate MeshInstance3Ds, so MeshDamaged's material_override does not reach them, and the light broken tone needs its own material. If the evidence expects exactly two material files, this can move into a sub_resource in the scene. | Frames vs files: after the round-4 render I rewrote the chunk rotations as exact matrices (7 decimals). The 3-decimal versions gave a derived node scale of about 1.0003, which a strict is_equal_approx(Vector3.ONE) check for 'never by node scale' would reject. Positions are unchanged and rotation entries moved by less than 0.0003, so there is no visible difference. Strictly, though, the delivered frames come from the render just before that edit. The existing Rubble chunks still use 3-decimal matrices (scale about 0.9999); I left them untouched as instructed. | 'Darker' holds measurably through albedo_color (0.55, then 0.38, then 0.26). But by design for this angle, the light scars raise the average brightness at play distance, so from above the damaged walls do not look much darker on average. Contrast, pattern and chips carry the read. | Art-director call: up close in the chase view, damaged 2 (and damaged 1 to a lesser degree) reads partly as a giraffe or crazed-lava print. Whether that still reads as 'the same wall, hurt' is an aesthetic decision, not mine. | Art-director call: the scar and chip tone is rust-orange, and Player 1's team colour is orange. I kept it darker and desaturated (red channel 0.55-0.64, well under the team orange), but it should be confirmed that it cannot be misread as a team marking at the teal Base. | Performance (no project budget was stated, so this is flagged, not measured): damaged 1 adds 4 chip draws per wall and damaged 2 adds 8. Only one look is visible at a time. In the worst case, all 8 Flag Walls in damaged 2 means 72 opaque draws instead of 8, per split-screen viewport. The chips cast no shadows. The detail layer adds 2 triplanar textures (6 extra samples per fragment), only on damaged panels. Texture memory is about 3.1 MB: two 512x512 RGBA8 masks with mipmaps and one 256x256. The masks are greyscale but stored as RGBA8 because of the colour ramp. Whether Forward+ batches the identical chips is unverified. | Round 3 (look-B/backup-r3/: damaged 1 albedo 0.40, cracks_1 frequency 0.0175 with ramp 0.05/0.085) gives a more natural damaged 1 in the chase view and more separation between the two looks, but damaged 1 is weaker from above. Swap those three values back if that trade is preferred. | The probe reports checks=0, so it verifies nothing itself. All structural claims above come from my own in-engine check (look-B/tools/geomcheck.gd, results in geomcheck2.txt). Separately, a headless -s run that called RenderingServer.shader_get_code hung and was killed. That happened only in my copy. | NoiseTexture2D generates its image on a thread at load. The damaged looks are hidden at start, so generation should finish long before they are shown, the same as for the existing scrap and garage textures. This was not measured on a slower machine or on Windows.
