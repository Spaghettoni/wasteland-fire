## Measured facts (the story's "Measure first")

1. **The track map holds a time over 1.** A SubViewport with `use_hdr_2d`, `transparent_bg` and `disable_3d` of 4096 x 1284 reads back as Image format 15 (RGBAH, half float). Stroke times of 100.5, 612.25 and 1500.0 s read back as 100.5, **612.0** and 1500.0: half-float steps are half a second at ten minutes, which a fade of 40 s cannot show. Without `use_hdr_2d` (format 5, RGBA8) the time clamps at 1.0 (`probes/probe1-hdr.txt`, `probe1-ldr.txt`).
2. **A colour is not a number in an HDR 2D target.** A stroke time written as the vertex `Color` of `draw_line` reads back as 56,224 (for 100.5) and 65,504, the half-float maximum (for 612.25 and 1500): the colour is converted from sRGB to linear and clamped (`probes/probe1-vertex-colour.txt`). So the brush carries the time in the UV, and its own colour is ignored.
3. **What was drawn stays; a clear clears.** A later `UPDATE_ONCE` keeps earlier strokes. A stroke drawn over another (at a crossing) replaces its time, as `blend_disabled` asks. `CLEAR_MODE_ONCE` with `UPDATE_ONCE` empties the map once; the property still reads ONCE afterwards, and a stroke drawn after that clear is kept. A stroke reads back on the frame it is drawn (`probes/probe2-metal.txt`, `probe2-vulkan.txt`).
4. **A global sampler takes the hints and the viewport's texture.** `global uniform sampler2D tyre_track_map : filter_linear, repeat_disable;` compiles. It takes a `ViewportTexture` through `RenderingServer.global_shader_parameter_set()`, and `textureSize()` returns the map's size. The 4.7 reference does not say whether a global sampler accepts a filter hint; it does (`probe1-hdr.txt`, the screen readback). A global uniform must be declared in `project.godot` before the shader is saved, as the reference says; `ProjectSettings.save_custom()` writes the `[shader_globals]` form used (`probes/probe-globals-saved.godot.txt`).
5. **The cost of an update.** With 8 strokes on every frame of a 4096 x 1284 half-float target: **0.127 ms of GPU and 0.043 ms of CPU** a frame on Vulkan; idle frames cost 0. Metal reports no GPU time (`probe2-vulkan.txt`, `probe2-metal.txt`).
6. **Tick order.** The Map is World's first child, so the node ticks before the Units and reads each one where it stood at the end of the tick before. World draws the Units between their last two ticks, so a track never runs ahead of the drawn tyre: `never_ahead`, windowed at 120 Hz (below).
7. **The draw order of sibling SubViewports** (the review's finding 3). Two sibling SubViewports, a 2D one recoloured every frame and a 3D one reading it through the global sampler: with the 2D one first in the tree, the 3D one read the colour of its own frame on all 42 frames judged; with it second, the colour of the frame before on all 42. The result was the same on Metal and on Vulkan (`probes/render-order-probe.gd.txt`, `render-order-probe.txt`). SplitScreen's World, which holds the Map and its track map, comes before its Views, so each view reads the map of its own frame.
8. **Two ticks in one frame** (the review's finding 1). With the physics at 120 ticks a second under `--fixed-fps 60`, a node that asks for a redraw on each tick has its `_draw()` run inside each tick (`Engine.is_in_physics_frame()` true, the same process frame), twice a frame (`probes/two-ticks-probe.gd.txt`, `two-ticks-probe.txt`). A redraw replaces the commands of the one before, so the track map got only the second tick's quads. The showcase at `--fixed-fps 30` (two ticks a frame), windowed, dumped dashed tracks (`measurements/two-ticks-a-frame-track-map-30fps-before-the-fix.png`). The fix redraws once a frame: the same drive then dumps a map byte-identical at 30 and at 60 fps (`measurements/two-ticks-a-frame-track-map-30fps-after-the-fix.png`, `measurements/two-ticks-a-frame-track-map-60fps.png`).
9. **A turn is a polygon.** A Unit turns and then moves on each tick, so in a steady turn its front tyres end each stroke up to 0.189 of the narrower tyre's width off the circle its rear tyres run on (`steady_turn`): one line to the eye, measured with a quarter of the width as the tolerance.
10. **The look's values**, from windowed recordings of the sand, the salt flat and the road in both views. At a darkness of 0.5 the track read pale on the bright salt flat, whose lit colour saturates there; at 0.8 it read clearly on the salt but heavy on the sand. 0.7 is between them, with a rut of 5 cm read 1.5 pixels to either side and 0.3 off the roughness. A hard-edged stroke stair-stepped along lines near the map's axes; a one-pixel soft rim centred on the tyre's edge removed it.
11. **Water and floors cover the ground.** The fords' water plane (at y 0.02) and the Garage floors (a plane at y 0.03, 12 x 10 m at Base-local (0, 0.03, 7), its open edge at local z 2) are other materials over the ground, so the marks the tyres leave there are hidden. The frames of the ford and the pad show it.
12. **Side faces.** `ALBEDO = mix(side_colour, colour, level)` already keeps a side face's colour, so the mark's `level` factor only changes a side face's roughness. The wall scene shows it in a frame (the wall reads 0.2141 against 0.2141 beside the track for Player 1 and 0.3395 against 0.3395 for Player 2, while on the ground in front of it the track is 0.285 and 0.302 darker than beside it).
13. **Far marks in the chase view** (the story's question: the track map has no mipmaps). The far scene lays the salt scene's circles, turns each Unit about, and rolls it back toward them in the chase view, filing two sets of four consecutive frames: at 3 to 4 m/s, then at 15 m/s (the Buggy) and 8 m/s (the Truck). In the retained frames (15 and 16, below) the circles' far arcs, 15 to 35 m from the camera, are thin lines a pixel or two wide with slight stair-stepping, much like the ground's own crack lines at that distance, and they keep their shape from frame to frame at both speeds. An earlier probe of the same scene put the marks 25 to 40 m away: there, at speed, the farthest arcs at the far right of a view turned dotted and varied a little between frames (`measurements/far-marks-25-to-40-m-earlier-probe-*.png`; that probe's drives touched props, which changes where the marks lie but not how a far mark is drawn). No fade was added; question 7 asks whether to add the story's fallback, a fade by the texel's size on screen.

## Review

One read-only pass by `godot-gdscript-specialist` (158k tokens) over `tyre_tracks.gd`, `tyre_track_painter.gd`, `tyre_track_settings.gd`, `tyre_contact.gd`, the brush shader, the ground shader's tracks lines, `map_field.gd`, SplitScreen's hand-off and the runner's strip, asked to read and not to edit (its report: `measurements/engine-review.txt`). It reported four findings and five areas with none.

1. **Strokes lost in a frame that holds two physics ticks: a real defect, fixed.** The painter asked for its redraw on each tick, and a deferred redraw runs inside each tick's flush; a CanvasItem's redraw replaces the commands of the one before, so a frame with two ticks drew only the second tick's quads. The reviewer reasoned it from memory of the main loop, so it was measured before anything changed (measured fact 8): `_draw()` ran twice a frame inside the ticks, and a windowed run at `--fixed-fps 30` dumped dashed tracks. Any frame rate under 60 would have dashed the tracks (a slower PC, a busy second, the jitter of a 60 Hz display); no check at `--fixed-fps 60` could see it. The painter now asks for its redraw and the viewport's update once a frame, in its own `_process()`, which also runs while the tree is paused. A tick only queues its quads. The new check `two_ticks_a_frame` (physics at 120 ticks a second under `--fixed-fps 60`: every one of 44 frames of two ticks drew all its strokes) is the only check that kills `redraw_on_every_tick`, the build before the fix, and `round_over_pause` now also asks that the strokes of the tick that ended the Round are drawn. Every other check printed the same numbers after the fix, and the 60 fps dump is byte-identical before and after it.
2. **The strength turned off by any node that leaves the tree: fixed another way.** The review's fix compares `RenderingServer.global_shader_parameter_get()` on leaving, which cannot work headless: the dummy renderer keeps no global parameters, the reason the `published` signal exists. A static holder does the same: the node that entered the tree last holds the parameters, and only it sets the strength to 0 when it leaves. A node that enters the tree again now sends every value again, as AC-7 says ("when it enters the tree"). `published` covers both, with the seeded defects `exit_turns_off_what_another_holds` and `no_publish_on_entering_again`.
3. **Old marks at full strength for a frame after R, if a view were drawn before the track map: measured, cannot happen in the shipped scene, guarded.** Sibling SubViewports are drawn in tree order (measured fact 7), and World, with the track map, comes before Views. No runtime change; the new check `drawn_first` holds the scene to that order, and `views_before_the_world` (the Views block moved above World) is killed by it. The check reads the tree order, which is the order the viewports enter on loading; a SubViewport added again at runtime would change the draw order without changing the tree order, and the check cannot see that.
4. **The tick order rests on the tree: left as built.** The review suggested a physics priority of -100. That would also move the node ahead of the input nodes at -1 (PlayerMatchInput's Self-destruct among them) and change what a Unit destroyed on its own tick draws. Being World's first child is the order `split_screen.gd` documents for every Map node. The seeded defect `tracks_tick_after_the_units` (the node's priority set after the Units') is killed by `lines` and `reverse`.

None found:
- **The unset global sampler on a Map with no node.** It has no NaN path: the default texture is finite, the denominators are floored, and every term is multiplied by a strength of 0.
- **The shader's math.** G over R holds under bilinear filtering, the fade line matches `strength_at()`, and the rut's sign and units are right.
- **The brush.** The four-point primitive is right, the UV interpolates exactly, and `blend_disabled` is valid.
- **The GDScript.**

The review's last four tool calls and its report came after a pause at the API limit; the decision log holds the four decisions.
