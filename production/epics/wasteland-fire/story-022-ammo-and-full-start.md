## Acceptance Criteria

*From the author's comments 7 and 9 and the developer's answers of 2026-10-10, scoped to this story:*

- [x] AC-1: Each Unit type carries a load of ammo from its data: Motorbike 100, Buggy 50, Truck 25 and Gyrocopter 40 Shots. Each Shot a Unit fires spends one of its load and nothing else changes: the fire key, the cadence, the damage, the range, the shooting height and the Shot itself are as before. With the fire key held from a full load, each type fires its whole load at its own cadence and then nothing more: the Motorbike 100 Shots 12 ticks apart, the Buggy 50 at 24, the Truck 25 at 48 and the Gyrocopter 40 at 30 (at 60 ticks per second), the last Shot 19.2 to 19.8 s after the first.
- [x] AC-2: A Unit with no Shots left fires nothing: no Shot while the key is held, none for a press, release and press again, none after any wait; until story 024 lands, ammo comes back only with a new Unit. Everything else works as with Shots left: the Unit drives and turns, a Motorbike picks up, carries and delivers a Flag, a Truck lays its Mines, and the Self-destruct and the Swap at the own Base work as before.
- [x] AC-3: Every new Unit starts with full supplies, a full tank (100 Fuel, every type) and a full load of ammo: at the Round's first spawn, at every respawn whatever destroyed the Unit (an enemy Shot, a Turret's Shot, a Mine, a Self-destruct, the Gyrocopter's Fuel crash and story 021's crash into a Tall piece) and after every Swap at the own Base, the same type chosen again included, so a Swap is a free full resupply and repair. A destroyed or benched Unit holds no Fuel and no Shots: a Unit that dies with Shots left does not carry them into its next life, and one that dies empty does not come back empty.
- [x] AC-4: While a Player's Unit is in play, that Player's view, and only that one, shows its Shots left as "Ammo 100 / 100", updated on the tick of every Shot and every refill and, for a new Unit, no later than the first tick it can fire. It behaves like the Truck's Mines line: shown while the Unit has a load (a capacity above 0), so hidden while the Player chooses or waits to respawn, and not hidden under the Round-over screen, where it keeps the count the Round ended with. It sits under the HUD band on the left, on the row of the Truck's Mines line, so a Truck shows "Ammo 25 / 25" and "Mines 5 / 5" side by side; it fits the 640 x 720 view with nothing clipped and covers no element of the HUD band, the Mines line, the notice line, the choice panel, the respawn countdown or the out-of-Fuel hint.
- [x] AC-5: On the tick a Unit fires its last Shot, and again at every new press of the fire key while its gun is empty, that Player's notice line, and only that Player's, shows "Out of ammo" for about 1.5 s, replacing whatever the line showed, as a refused Mine lay does; a key held down shows it once, not once per tick, and the line hides when the Round ends and at R. The out-of-Fuel hint, the Mines line and the "Destroy the turrets first" notice work as before.
- [x] AC-6: The Turrets fire as before, with no count: a Turret firing at a target for longer than any Unit's load keeps firing (R2). The Mines stay separate (R3): a Shot never takes a Mine and a lay never spends a Shot; a Truck that has fired its last Shot still lays its Mines, one that has laid its last Mine still fires, and every new Truck still brings 5 Mines.
- [x] AC-7: Every value is data: each type's load in its UnitStats `.tres` (100, 50, 25 and 40; a type without the line, 0, fires with no count, as before this story); the full start in the same files (the share of the tank a new Unit starts with, 1.0 for every type, the knob kept); the texts ("Ammo %d / %d" and "Out of ammo") in their scenes, through `tr()`, naming no key; the notice's time in `player_notice.tscn`. No number is a constant in code. The evidence scenarios written before this story run on the rules they were measured with (unlimited Shots, the half tank, no Ammo line, the notice line only where their own opt-ins kept it), as does every scenario that does not declare this story's opt-in `AMMO`, and keep their outputs byte for byte against the capture taken twice at the start of this story (Implementation Notes, "The older outputs"), but for exact rewrites pinned in `normalizers.json`; each strip is proven by a seeded "runner leaves it on" defect, or recorded as not load-bearing where no older output can tell it.
